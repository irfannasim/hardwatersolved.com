**Aquasure vs SpringWell** comes down to box price. The SpringWell SS4 costs far more than the Aquasure Harmony 48k at each brand's own store. Both ship a 48,000-grain softener in a 10 x 54 inch tank, and both need the same shutoffs, fittings, air gap and salt before they soften anything. SpringWell also sells the bypass separately, so installed yourself, the gap stays about as wide as it is in the box. The extra money buys SpringWell's longer warranty, a six-month return window and a higher flow rating. It does not buy more resin, a standard valve or a certification. For a careful DIY installer on city water, the Aquasure is the better value.

Two lines matter. **The bypass:** Aquasure includes one; SpringWell adds it to the cart by default, and you should keep it, because without a bypass every service job means shutting off the house. **The brine line:** SpringWell's list does not mention one. Check the box on delivery before you start, so you are not stopped half way through an install.

Neither list mentions a brine tank safety float. If the brine tank sits anywhere a slow overflow would do damage, ask the seller whether one is fitted, and plumb the overflow elbow to a drain.

## What You Still Have to Buy

Both softeners need the same site parts. The costs are illustrative DIY estimates.

| Item | Why | Aquasure | SpringWell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bypass valve | Isolate the softener for service | Included | Extra, sold by SpringWell |
| Salt at start-up | Aquasure's manual asks for 80 lb; SpringWell's guide says 4 to 5 bags | About $20 | About $40 to $50 |
| Shutoffs, pipe and fittings | Reach the 1-inch connections | About $60 to $150 | About $60 to $150 |
| Drain air gap | Code and both manuals; never a direct connection to a waste pipe | About $15 to $30 | About $15 to $30 |
| Grounding jumper | Plastic valves break continuity on copper or galvanized pipe | About $15 | About $15 |
| Pressure-reducing valve | Only if house pressure exceeds 80 psi | Usually $0 | Usually $0, but SpringWell's limit is 80 psi |
| Carbon prefilter | Chlorine over 2 ppm (Aquasure's limit), or chloramine | Extra; Aquasure sells a prefilter bundle | Extra; SpringWell sells a filter-plus-softener combo in the SS4 size |

The pressure line is easy to miss. SpringWell rates the SS range for **25 to 80 psi**. Aquasure's valve takes up to 125 psi, though its manual also asks for a pressure-reducing valve above 80 psi. If your static pressure reads above 80, budget for a regulator either way.

Most DIY failures with either box happen in the same places: the drain connected without an air gap, solder heat too close to a plastic valve, and inlet and outlet reversed. The list of [common water softener installation mistakes](/blog/common-water-softener-installation-mistakes/) covers each one, with the symptom it causes.

*Labor is the national range from Fixr, as used in the site's [water softener installation cost](/blog/water-softener-installation-cost/) breakdown. A house that needs a new loop, drain run or outlet adds the same amount to either softener.*

The labor and parts cost the same whichever box is on the floor, so the gap is almost all box price, whether you install it yourself or not. As a share of the total, though, it shrinks when a plumber is involved, because the labor adds the same to both.

## Capacity: Both Are Sold, and Programmed, on the Box Number

The tanks are the same size and the grain ratings are the same. Neither brand publishes capacity at a stated salt dose. Using standard per-cubic-foot design figures for about 1.5 cubic feet of resin, both deliver roughly:

| Salt dose | Capacity |
|---|---|
| 6 lb per cu ft | about 31,500 grains |
| 9 lb per cu ft | about 39,000 grains |
| 15 lb per cu ft | about 46,500 grains |

*Standard resin in good condition. The method is in [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/).*

Both setup guides use the 48,000-grain box rating. Aquasure's manual has you divide capacity by hardness and subtract 75 gallons per person. SpringWell's guide has you enter "48" with a 10% reserve. Either way, if the brine fill delivers a moderate dose, the valve waits too long and the house gets hard water at the end of each cycle. On either softener, program from about 39,000 grains, then test the water on the morning before a regeneration and adjust.

Neither publishes the refill flow rate, so neither manual tells you the salt dose in pounds. Each gallon of refill dissolves roughly 3 lb of salt. Measure the brine tank's water rise after a regeneration to find yours.

## The Valve and the Support Line

Neither softener uses a Fleck or Clack valve, the two families most plumbers and suppliers stock.

- **Aquasure's Aquatrol** is Aquasure's own design. Parts come from Aquasure, and it programs from the keypad. Aquasure advertises free US-based technical support for registered products.
- **SpringWell's head** is an app-programmed Bluetooth valve. SpringWell does not name its maker; a dealer review identifies it as a Chandler Systems Connected Series valve. Bypass and parts come from SpringWell, and there is no keypad programming in the guide.

So with either, the brand's support line is part of the product. SpringWell's higher price buys phone support and a longer warranty behind that line. Aquasure's buys you the same dependence on one supplier for much less.

SpringWell's terms are longer on paper. Two cautions apply. The 7-year versus 5-year electronics discrepancy has appeared before, so ask SpringWell which applies and keep the answer. And the six-month window covers **uninstalled** units only. Once it is plumbed in, SpringWell's "Performance Promise" offers troubleshooting and parts, and refunds only systems it finds defective.

Aquasure's main trap is the 60-day registration. Miss it and the five years becomes one. Its iron and chlorine exclusion also means a Harmony used on an iron well, or on high-chlorine city water without a prefilter, has no resin or valve-internals cover.

## Which One to Buy

**Buy the Aquasure Harmony 48k if** you will install it yourself, you are on city water with chlorine under 2 ppm (or will add the prefilter), and you will register it within 60 days. It is the lower-cost complete two-tank kit here, and the money saved pays for a plumber's afternoon if you get stuck.

**Buy the SpringWell SS4 if** you value a lifetime defects warranty on the tank, valve and head, want a 13 gpm flow rating for a larger house, and are happy to program from a phone app. Confirm the resin grade and the electronics term in writing before you order.

**Spend the difference on water treatment instead if** you are on a well with iron or manganese. Both warranties exclude what that water does to the resin. An iron filter ahead of the cheaper softener protects it better than a longer warranty that excludes the damage.

## Who Should Buy Neither

- **Anyone who wants a valve any local technician can service.** Look at softeners on a Fleck or Clack valve.
- **Buyers who need certified performance.** Neither salt-based softener shows an NSF/ANSI 44 listing.
- **Homes that do not need ion exchange.** If your goal is scale reduction on moderate hardness, or you cannot run a drain line, [salt-based vs salt-free water treatment](/blog/salt-based-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/) compares the honest alternatives.
- **Anyone who will not adjust the default programming.** Both guides start from the box rating; an installer who sets it up for you is worth paying for.
