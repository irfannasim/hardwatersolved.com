**The Whirlpool vs GE water softener choice is closer than the boxes suggest, and it flips depending on what your house needs.** Compared at the same salt dose of about 10 lb, GE's current GXSH35W and GXSH45W deliver slightly more soft water than Whirlpool's WHES33 and WHES48. Whirlpool's top model flows more, at 12 gpm, and tolerates more iron, up to 12 ppm. GE's include Wi-Fi alerts and a first-year warranty with labor from its own service network. Where you can buy them has changed too. On 2 October 2026 GE's 2025 range was listed on Amazon, while Whirlpool's softeners were not.

## Whirlpool vs GE Water Softener: Three Matched Pairs

Both brands sell single-tank cabinet softeners, with the resin tank inside the salt cabinet. Both name each model by its capacity at the **heaviest** salt dose. GE's GXSH45W reaches its 55,424-grain maximum only at 25.4 lb of salt per regeneration, while Whirlpool's WHES48 reaches 48,000 at 18.1 lb. So the box numbers cannot be compared directly.

The fair comparison restates both at the same dose. The 10 lb column below is interpolated in a straight line between each maker's two nearest published points. The real capacity curve bows above that line, so these estimates lean conservative.

| Tier | Model | Resin | Published capacity | At about 10 lb salt (est.) | Box rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~30,000 | Whirlpool WHES30 | 0.82 cu ft | 26,600 @ 7.8 lb; 31,700 @ 13.1 lb | ~28,700 | 30,000 |
| ~30,000 | GE GXMD30W | 0.9 cu ft + carbon | 27,207 @ 8.8 lb; 30,899 @ 15.3 lb | ~27,900 | 30,000 |
| ~35,000 | Whirlpool WHES33 | 0.88 cu ft | 28,500 @ 8.3 lb; 34,100 @ 14.1 lb | ~30,100 | 33,000 |
| ~35,000 | GE GXSH35W | 1.0 cu ft | 32,636 @ 9.8 lb; 37,065 @ 17.1 lb | ~32,800 | 35,000 |
| ~45,000+ | Whirlpool WHES48 | 1.33 cu ft | 14,200 @ 2.8 lb; 37,900 @ 10.5 lb | ~36,400 | 48,000 |
| ~45,000+ | GE GXSH45W | 1.5 cu ft | 39,364 @ 9.8 lb; 55,424 @ 25.4 lb | ~39,600 | 45,000 |

*Published figures from each maker's manual or product page. GE's manual notes that its ratings are certified by IAPMO R&T only at the highest and lowest salt doses shown, so its middle figures are GE's own.*

What the pairs show:

- **At the 30,000 tier, they are even.** The GXMD30W gives up a little resin to fit a carbon stage, certified to NSF/ANSI 42 for chlorine taste and odour. The WHES30 is the plainer, cheaper-to-run softener.
- **At the middle tier, GE leads by about 9%.** The GXSH35W carries more resin than the WHES33, 1.0 against 0.88 cubic feet.
- **At the top tier, the names mislead.** The "48,000" Whirlpool delivers less than the "45,000" GE at a normal dose, because GE's resin bed is bigger. The WHES48 wins back ground on flow and iron.

How the salt dose changes capacity, and why a box rating is a ceiling, is explained in [water softener salt efficiency](/blog/water-softener-salt-efficiency/). Both brands are very efficient at their minimum dose: GE's GXSH35W and GXSH45W are rated at 5,120 grains per pound of salt, and Whirlpool's WHES48 at 5,090.

## Flow, Hardness and Iron Limits

![Washing machine in a home laundry corner, one of the fixtures that adds to the peak flow a Whirlpool or GE cabinet softener has to carry](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4993073/pexels-photo-4993073.jpeg)

Capacity decides how often a softener regenerates. These limits decide whether it suits the house at all.

| Model | Rated service flow | Max hardness | Max clear-water iron |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whirlpool WHES30 | 7.6 gpm | 95 gpg | 8 ppm |
| GE GXMD30W | 8 gpm | 165 gpg | 5 ppm |
| Whirlpool WHES33 | 8 gpm | 105 gpg | 9 ppm |
| GE GXSH35W | 8 gpm | 165 gpg | 5 ppm |
| Whirlpool WHES48 | 12 gpm | 160 gpg | 12 ppm |
| GE GXSH45W | 9.6 gpm | 165 gpg | 5 ppm |

*Whirlpool flows from NSF's listing; GE flows from its manual. GE's specification page and manual disagree on some 2025 iron limits; we use the manual's 5 ppm.*

Two differences matter in practice.

**Flow at the top tier.** A three-bathroom house with two showers and a washer running can pass 8 gpm. The WHES48's 12 gpm rating gives real headroom; the GXSH45W's 9.6 gpm gives some. Below the top tier, both brands sit at about 8 gpm.

**Iron on a well.** Whirlpool rates the WHES30 for 8 ppm of clear-water iron, rising to 12 ppm on the WHES44 and WHES48; only its compact WHES18 and hybrid WHESFC stop at 3 ppm. GE's 2025 range is rated at 5 ppm. If a lab test shows ferrous iron between 5 and 12 ppm, Whirlpool is the only one of the two built for it. Above any model's rating, an iron filter comes first.

## The Warranties, Quoted

The two brands look alike on the box and differ most on paper.

**GE**, from the current owner's manual:

| Period from purchase | GE replaces | Labor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | "Any part of the Water Softening System which fails due to defect in materials or workmanship" | Included: GE Appliances "will also provide, free of charge, all labor and related service" |
| 3 years | "The electronic monitor" | You pay |
| 10 years | "A replacement resin tank" | You pay |

Service runs through GE's factory service centres or an authorised technician. The manual cuts cover to 90 days for anything other than private family use. There is no cleanser condition and no extension scheme.

**Whirlpool**, from the WHES30 and WHES33 manual. The warrantor is Water Channel Partners, not Whirlpool Corporation:

| Period from delivery | Covered | Labor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | "All parts will be free from defects in materials and workmanship" | Included |
| 3 years | "The electronic control board" | Not covered after year 1 |
| 10 years | "The salt storage tank and fiberglass resin/mineral tank" | Not covered after year 1 |

The full warranty "can be extended to five (5) years from the date the product is delivered if you use Whirlpool WHE-WSC Water Softener Cleaner on your system", one bottle every four months, with proof of purchase kept. "Use of any water softener additive other than Whirlpool WHE-WSC will not provide extended warranty coverage." PRO models such as the WHES48 start at 2 years and extend to 10 on the same terms. After the first year, the warrantor ships replacement parts to your home and you fit them. Use of aftermarket parts voids all warranties.

The practical difference: GE includes a technician visit in year one, and its terms do not depend on what you buy afterwards. Whirlpool offers a much longer full warranty, but only if you keep buying its cleanser three times a year. Reading conditional extensions like this one is covered in [how to read a water softener warranty](/blog/how-to-read-a-water-softener-warranty/).

## Parts and Service

**Whirlpool.** Whirlpool's water site sells the wear parts under its own part numbers, including standard and high-flow rotor and seal kits, the drive motor, the 24 V power supply and bypass valves. Support runs through Water Channel Partners' phone line, not Whirlpool's appliance service. Third-party kits sold online as fitting Whirlpool, GE, Kenmore and Morton units exist, but they void Whirlpool's warranty.

**GE.** GE's manuals list part numbers for the nozzle and venturi, seals and control parts, and warranty service runs through GE Appliances' factory network. GE's own troubleshooting table names the nozzle and venturi most often for no soft water, ahead of the drain hose and loose control wiring. For an older obsolete model, ask GE Appliances whether its parts are still stocked before you rely on it.

Both brands' valves are proprietary to their makers, unlike the Fleck and Clack valves on many two-tank systems. Error codes and blank displays on either brand are diagnosed in [water softener error code or blank display](/blog/water-softener-error-code-or-blank-display/).

## Lifespan: What the Warranties Imply

Neither brand publishes a service life. Both warranties point the same way: tanks for 10 years, electronics for 3, and moving parts for 1 or 2 unless extended. The general pattern, and how water quality moves it, is in [how long does a water softener last](/blog/how-long-does-a-water-softener-last/).

Three things shorten either brand's life:

- **Running at the iron limit.** Iron near a model's rating fouls resin and seals faster. GE's 5 ppm and Whirlpool's 8 to 12 ppm are maximums, not comfortable operating points.
- **Chlorinated city water.** GE's manual warns that resin may degrade in chlorine above 2 ppm.
- **Block or rock salt.** Whirlpool recommends evaporated pellets or solar crystals and advises against block salt.

## Smart Features

GE's 2025 models build in Wi-Fi through the SmartHQ app: low-salt reminders, alerts for low, medium or high continuous flow, and vacation mode. A 3 a.m. continuous-flow alert usually means a running toilet or a leak. Whole-house shut-off works only with GE's separately sold valve.

Whirlpool's WHES range has no Wi-Fi. Its Wi-Fi model is the WHEC46, rated at 12.5 gpm, and Whirlpool sells a separate remote Wi-Fi shut-off valve. If alerts are the reason you are buying, GE gives them across the range; Whirlpool gives them on one model.

## Which to Choose

- **Busiest moment above about 8 gpm:** the Whirlpool WHES48 at 12 gpm, from a store. The GE GXSH45W at 9.6 gpm is the next best. Above about 10 gpm, a two-tank system on a larger valve fits better, as covered in [single-tank vs twin-tank water softeners](/blog/single-tank-vs-twin-tank-water-softeners/).
- **Well water with 5 to 12 ppm of clear-water iron:** Whirlpool WHES44 or WHES48. GE's 2025 range stops at 5 ppm.
- **City water, want alerts or Amazon delivery:** the GE GXSH35W, or the GXMD30W if chlorine taste bothers you.
- **None of those applies:** buy whichever is cheaper at the same tier, compared at about 10 lb of salt, and keep the receipt for the warranty.

If you have not yet decided whether to soften at all, start with [salt-based vs salt-free water treatment](/blog/salt-based-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/).

## Who Should Buy Neither

- **Large households with heavy overlapping use.** Both brands are cabinet units built around about 1 to 1.5 cubic feet of resin. A two-tank system with a bigger bed and valve serves them better.
- **Wells with iron above 12 ppm, any ferric iron, or a sulfur smell.** Neither softener is the fix. Filter first.
- **Rental or small-business use.** GE's cover falls to 90 days for anything but private family use.
- **Anyone who wants a valve any technician can service.** Both use proprietary valves supported by one maker each.
- **Owners who will not buy cleanser but want Whirlpool's long warranty.** Without the WHE-WSC schedule, the full cover ends after one year, or two on PRO models.

The verdict on Whirlpool vs GE water softeners: at the same salt dose GE's current units deliver a little more soft water and come with Wi-Fi and a first-year labor warranty. Whirlpool's WHES48 is the one to buy for higher flow or more iron. Compare them on the shelf at matched tiers, not by the numbers on the boxes.
