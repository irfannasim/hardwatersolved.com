If hardness is above 15 gpg, iron above 3 ppm or alkalinity above 250 ppm, buy separate tanks instead: a softener first and a tannin tank after it.

Before buying either, spend two minutes confirming that the colour really is tannin. Iron causes the same yellow water, and a tannin filter does nothing for it.

## First, Confirm It Is Tannin

![Clear glass of water standing on a wooden table, the setup for the 24-hour glass test that tells tannin colour from iron](https://images.pexels.com/photos/35592218/pexels-photo-35592218.jpeg)

Draw a glass from a cold tap after running it for two minutes. Cover it and leave it out of sunlight for 24 hours:

- **Colour settles to the bottom as orange or brown sediment:** iron. A tannin filter will not help.
- **Black or brown-black specks settle out:** manganese.
- **Colour stays evenly spread, like weak tea, with nothing on the bottom:** tannins.

Then pour some through a white coffee filter. Iron stains the paper. Tannin passes through and the water stays yellow. Both tests, with what to do when the result is mixed, are set out in [why softened water looks yellow](/blog/why-softened-water-looks-yellow/).

If the glass says tannin, get a lab test before you buy. You need five numbers, not one:

| Result | Why it matters for the purchase |
|---|---|
| Tannin (ppm) | The combo units here are rated to 3 ppm |
| Hardness (gpg) | Over 15 gpg rules out a combo tank |
| Iron (ppm) | Over 3 ppm rules out a combo tank and fouls tannin resin |
| Alkalinity (ppm) | Over 250 ppm rules out a combo; under about 50 ppm warns of a pH drop |
| pH | Below about 6.8 needs correcting before the resin |

## Combo Tank or Separate Tanks: What Actually Differs

A combination unit blends tannin (anion) resin and softening (cation) resin in one tank under one valve. A separate system puts a softener first and a tannin tank after it. Both regenerate with salt. They differ in four ways.

| | Combo tank | Separate tanks |
|---|---|---|
| **Tannin capacity** | Fixed by the anion resin in the blend: 0.5 cu ft in every AFWFilters size | Set by the tannin tank: 1.0 cu ft or more is common |
| **Protection of the tannin resin** | None: every bead sees raw hardness and iron | The softener removes hardness and some iron first |
| **Salt** | Whole bed brined on the tannin schedule, every 3 days or less | Softener on its own schedule; only the tannin tank every 3 days |
| **Replacing worn tannin resin** | The resins are blended, so the whole tank is rebedded | Replace only the tannin resin |
| **Space and cost** | One tank, one valve, one drain | Two tanks, two valves, more floor space |

The limits for a combo tank come from Urbans Aqua, an anion resin supplier. Its [anion resin guidance](https://urbansaqua.com/products/ion-exchange-resins/anion-resin-overview/) says raw water hardness "should not exceed 15 grains per gallon and alkalinity should not exceed 250 ppm," and that a dual bed "is not recommended when iron is greater than 3 ppm." Treat those as one supplier's figures and ask any seller for the limits in its own manual.

### The tannin schedule decides the size

Tannin resin has to be regenerated at least every three days, according to Urbans Aqua. In a combo tank the whole bed regenerates on that schedule. That changes how much softening resin you need: enough for three days of hardness, not the week or more a softener alone is sized for.

*Illustrative: 60 gallons per person per day, regenerating every three days.*

| Household and hardness | Hardness load per 3 days | Smallest AFWFilters combo that covers it |
|---|---|---|
| 2 people, 10 gpg | about 3,600 grains | 1.0 cu ft (16,000 grains) |
| 4 people, 15 gpg | about 10,800 grains | 1.0 cu ft (16,000 grains) |
| 6 people, 15 gpg | about 16,200 grains | 1.5 cu ft (32,000 grains) |

For most families inside the combo limits, the 1.0 cubic foot unit is enough. Buy the larger sizes for their flow rate, if your home's peak use needs more than 8 gpm, rather than for capacity you will regenerate away unused every three days.

## Salt Use Compared

Urbans Aqua puts the dose at no more than 10 lb of salt per cubic foot of tannin resin, with regeneration at least every three days. On those figures, for a four-person home at 15 gpg:

| Setup | Regenerations a year | Salt per regeneration | Salt a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combo, 1.0 cu ft, every 3 days | about 122 | about 10 lb | about 1,220 lb |
| Combo, 1.5 cu ft, every 3 days | about 122 | about 15 lb | about 1,830 lb |
| Separate: 1.5 cu ft softener every 7 days at 6 lb/cu ft, plus 1.0 cu ft tannin tank every 3 days | about 52 + 122 | 9 lb + 10 lb | about 1,690 lb |

*Illustrative, assuming the whole bed is dosed at the tannin rate. Your valve settings and water will change these numbers.*

The pattern holds even if your numbers differ. Tannin removal is salt-hungry whichever layout you pick. A correctly sized combo uses the least salt but has the least tannin resin. The separate layout holds twice the tannin resin for slightly less salt than an oversized combo.

## Settings That Keep Any Tannin Filter Working

Most tannin systems that "stop working" after a few months are under-regenerated. Whichever you buy:

- **Regenerate at least every three days,** even if a meter says the softening resin has capacity left.
- **Use a long brine contact time.** Urbans Aqua gives 30 minutes as the minimum.
- **Watch the colour.** Urbans Aqua calls returning colour "the best indication of a fouled resin bed."
- **Keep chlorine off the resin.** If the well is shock chlorinated, bypass the system until the chlorine is flushed out.

Why anion resin behaves this way, and how it can pull pH down early in each run on soft, low-alkalinity water, is explained in [can a water softener remove tannins](/blog/can-a-water-softener-remove-tannins/). Where a tannin unit sits in a full well treatment train is covered in [water softener for well water](/blog/water-softener-for-well-water/).

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **The glass test shows sediment.** That is iron or manganese, and an iron filter is the purchase.
- **The yellow is only at hot taps.** Look at the water heater, not the well.
- **pH is below about 6.8 with low alkalinity.** Correct the pH first with a neutralizer, then soften, then remove tannin. A combo tank on that water can push pH lower still.
- **The colour bothers you only in drinking water.** A reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen tap removes it for far less than whole-house anion resin.
- **Your water is above pH 8 and chlorinated.** Anion resin suppliers warn of a fishy odour in that combination. Ask for a water analysis review before buying.

For most tea-coloured wells within the combo limits, the AFWFilters 1 cu ft digital tannin softener does the job with the least salt. Above those limits, keep or buy a softener and put a separate tannin tank after it. Either way, buy on the tannin resin volume and regenerate every three days.
