This **Pelican water softener review** has to start with availability, because the answer to "which Pelican should I buy?" changed this year. On 1 October 2026, every Pelican and Pentair-branded softener and NaturSoft listing we checked on Amazon showed "Currently unavailable", and pelicanwater.com was refusing connections. On paper the two lines were good at different jobs. The salt-based unit, sold on Amazon as the Pentair 35921, is a 48,600-grain softener that removes hardness. NaturSoft is a salt-free conditioner that leaves hardness in and aims to stop scale. Pick the line your water test points to, then buy the equivalent that is actually for sale.

## The Two Pelican Lines Side by Side

Pelican marketed both products to the same buyer, someone tired of scale, and the names did not help. Here is what each one was.

| | Pentair 35921 (salt-based) | Pelican NaturSoft NS3-P / NS6-P (salt-free) |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Removes calcium and magnesium by ion exchange | Leaves them in; conditions them to reduce scale |
| Capacity or range | 48,600 grains; 33,500 and 64,000 also offered | Rated to 75 gpg hardness |
| Sized for | 3 to 4 bathrooms | NS3: 1 to 3 bathrooms; NS6: 4 to 6 |
| Salt and drain | Needs salt, a drain and power | No salt, no drain, no electricity |
| Notable hardware | Self-cleaning brine injector, single rotary disc seal | Stainless steel exterior; sediment prefilter |
| Maintenance | Salt top-ups; occasional injector and seal service | Prefilter every 6 to 9 months; first-tank media every 5 years or 600,000 gallons |
| Water it needs | Tolerates small amounts of dissolved iron | Iron, manganese, sulfide and tannins removed first |
| Hardness test afterward | Near zero | Unchanged |
| Warranty | Not captured from the listing | 12-year limited on tanks, valves, heads, bypass and housings |

The NaturSoft column is not a weaker softener. It is a different product. Pentair's own listing called it a "water softener alternative", and the honest version of that phrase is in our explainer on [whether a salt-free system actually softens water](/blog/does-a-salt-free-system-actually-soften-water/). It does not. It can reduce scale in heated equipment, which is a narrower and still useful job.

## Which Pelican Line Your Water Test Points To

The brand's two products overlap in marketing, not in what they do. Your water test decides between them.

![Decision chart for choosing between the Pelican salt-based softener and NaturSoft salt-free conditioner from a water test, with Amazon alternatives for each](/diagrams/pelican-salt-based-vs-natursoft-water-test-decision.svg "Hardness, contaminants and what you want from the water decide the line; availability decides the brand")

**Start with an independent test.** Penn State Extension's guide to [water softening](https://extension.psu.edu/water-softening) advises a third-party lab rather than a seller's free hardness test, and notes that water under about 7 gpg will probably not cause major scaling or soap film. Below that level, neither Pelican product is likely to be worth its price.

**Then check for the things that break NaturSoft.** Iron, manganese, hydrogen sulfide and tannins all have to be removed before the NaturSoft tanks. A salt-based softener handles a little dissolved iron as long as its sizing allows for it, as explained in [testing iron and manganese before sizing](/blog/test-iron-and-manganese-before-sizing/). A salt-free conditioner simply fouls.

**Then decide what you want from the water.**

- If you want less soap, no soap scum, softer-feeling laundry and a hardness test near zero, you want the salt-based line. Only ion exchange does those things.
- If you want less scale in the water heater and on fixtures, with no salt, no drain and no sodium added, and your hardness is within range, the salt-free line fits. Our page on [when a salt-free conditioner is wrong](/blog/when-a-salt-free-conditioner-is-wrong/) lists the expectations it will not meet.

**Above 75 gpg**, NaturSoft is out of its rated range, and most salt-free conditioners perform best well below their maximum. The [hardness limits for salt-free conditioners](/blog/hardness-limits-for-salt-free-conditioners/) explain why spec-sheet maximums run ahead of real-world results.

## The Salt-Based Pelican on Paper

The Amazon listing for the Pentair 35921 describes an on-demand, metered two-tank softener rated at 48,600 grains for three to four bathrooms. Its two named features are a self-cleaning brine injector and a single rotary disc seal. Both target the most common service calls on any softener: a plugged injector that stops brine draw, and worn piston seals that leak hard water into the service line.

What the listing does not give is just as important. There is no resin volume in cubic feet, no salt dose behind the 48,600 figure, no flow rate and no warranty text. Without resin volume you cannot restate the grain rating at a realistic salt setting. A typical softener sold at about 48,000 grains holds about 1.5 cubic feet of resin. At an efficient salt dose that delivers nearer 31,500 grains than 48,000, as our guide to [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/) shows. If you are offered a 35921 by a dealer, ask for the resin volume and the capacity at the salt dose they will programme.

## NaturSoft on Paper

NaturSoft is a two-tank salt-free system. The listings and warranty text describe:

- **A sediment prefilter**, to be changed every 6 to 9 months.
- **A first tank** whose media lasts about 5 years or 600,000 gallons, then needs replacing.
- **A second tank** with media described as lifetime.
- **A rated maximum of 75 gpg** hardness and, on the NS3-P listing, a 10 gpm maximum flow.
- **A 12-year limited warranty** on tanks, valves, heads, bypass and housings.

The listings say you will still see some spotting, but that it wipes off more easily. That is an honest description of salt-free conditioning. The scale-forming minerals are still there; they are just less likely to stick.

The running cost is low but not zero: a prefilter cartridge roughly twice a year and a first-tank media change every five years or so. Our guide to [salt-free conditioner maintenance](/blog/salt-free-water-conditioner-maintenance/) works through that cost against a softener's salt bill.

## Certification Checks

**Salt-based line.** NSF/ANSI 44 is the standard for ion-exchange softeners. A search of NSF's public listings, current as of 30 September 2026, found [no NSF/ANSI 44 listing under the Pelican trade name](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/DWTU/Listings.asp?TradeName=Pelican&Standard=044), and none for the 35921 model number. That does not prove the unit performs poorly. It means its capacity and efficiency have not been verified by NSF.

**NaturSoft.** NSF/ANSI 44 does not apply, because NaturSoft does not exchange ions. Its listings claim it is the "only water softener alternative certified 99.6% effective by DVGW-9191". DVGW is the German water association, and its W 512 protocol is the recognised test for scale-prevention devices. The listing wording does not identify a W 512 certificate number, so we could not check it. Treat the 99.6 percent as a manufacturer claim. Our guide to [verifying a softener certification](/blog/how-to-verify-a-softener-certification/) shows how to look up a certificate when a seller does give one.

## If You Already Own a Pelican

- **Keep your proof of purchase and serial number.** Warranty claims on a brand in transition depend on paperwork.
- **Contact Pentair's residential support** for warranty and parts questions, since Pelican was a Pentair brand.
- **NaturSoft owners:** keep changing the sediment prefilter on schedule, and budget for the first-tank media change at about five years. Ask your Pentair dealer now whether that media is still supplied.
- **Salt-based owners:** injectors, seals and brine parts are the routine service items. A local water-treatment dealer can tell you whether parts for your valve are stocked.

## Who Pelican Is Wrong For Now

- **Anyone who needs to buy this month.** There is no active Amazon offer and no working Pelican web store.
- **Anyone who wants soft water from NaturSoft.** It will not lower a hardness test, save soap or end soap scum.
- **Wells with iron, manganese, sulfide or tannins**, unless they are treated first, especially for NaturSoft.
- **Buyers who need verified performance.** Neither line had a certification we could confirm.
- **Anyone who cannot get written warranty and parts commitments** from the seller of a remaining unit.

Pelican's real contribution was offering both answers under one roof. You still need to answer the question first: soft water or less scale. If that is the decision you are stuck on, read [salt-based vs salt-free water treatment](/blog/salt-based-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/), then choose from what is actually on sale.
