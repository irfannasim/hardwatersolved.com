## What the Certification Covers, and Who Issued It

The word "certified" on a countertop RO box can mean three quite different things. This is what we found for each unit:

| Unit | Who certified it | Standards | What the listing covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| AquaTru Classic | IAPMO R&T | NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 | AquaTru counts 84 contaminants. The certified claims include lead, fluoride, PFOA/PFOS and microplastics. A directory check found chlorine marketed but not among the certified claims |
| APEC ROCT-C1 | Not named | APEC's store titles the replacement filter "NSF 372 & 58 Certified" | We could not find which body certified it or which contaminant claims are listed |
| APEC ROCT-PLUS | None stated | None on APEC's product page or the Amazon listing | APEC lists more than 50 contaminants it says the unit removes. None is tied to a certification |
| Waterdrop K19-H *(not ranked)* | — | Listing title claims NSF/ANSI 372 only | Lead content of the parts. Says nothing about what the unit removes |

Read them like this. **NSF/ANSI 58** is the reverse osmosis standard. It covers TDS reduction and any contaminants listed one by one. **42** and **53** cover the carbon stages, for taste and odour and for health contaminants respectively. **401** covers emerging contaminants such as some pharmaceuticals. **372** is a materials standard for lead content and nothing more.

Certification belongs to a model, so look up the exact model. NSF, IAPMO and WQA each publish searchable listings. Treat a claim you cannot find on any of them as a marketing claim.

## How Often You Will Refill It

Countertop RO is manual. You fill a tap-water tank at the sink, and the unit turns it into treated water. How much treated water one fill makes sets how often you do this:

The daily volumes are illustrative. Count the water your household drinks, plus coffee, tea, rice and pasta, and any pet bowls you fill from it. A family that cooks with RO water reaches 2 gallons a day quickly. At four fills a day, the AquaTru is still workable, but it becomes a chore. ## What Countertop RO Fixes, and What It Does Not

A countertop unit treats the water you pour from it. That is a real fix for some hard-water complaints and no fix at all for others.

**It fixes:**
- Hardness, sodium and most dissolved solids in drinking and cooking water
- Scale in the kettle, coffee maker and steam iron, if you fill them from the RO tank
- Taste and odour from chlorine, through the carbon stages

**It does not fix:**
- Scale on faucets, showerheads and glass doors
- Scale in the water heater and dishwasher
- Dry skin or soap scum in the shower
- Anything in the water before you pour it, such as the hot water from your tap

Those are whole-house problems, and in a rental the answer runs through the landlord. ## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Renters whose landlord allows an under-sink unit.** A plumbed RO unit makes water on demand with no refilling and no counter space.
- **Households using more than about 3 gallons a day.** Refilling becomes the main chore of the kitchen.
- **Anyone whose real complaint is scale on fixtures** or the water heater. That calls for a softener. If you own the place later, [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/) starts from your hardness and water use.
- **Untreated well water or a boil-water notice.** AquaTru's limits call for microbiologically safe feed water. RO is not a disinfection system.
- **Anyone with no counter space** near an outlet. The AquaTru alone takes a 12 × 14 inch footprint.

For a renter with hard tap water, a countertop RO unit is the most complete drinking-water fix that needs no permission. Pick the AquaTru Classic if certification matters most. Pick the APEC ROCT-PLUS if refills and filter upkeep matter more, or the ROCT-C1 if you want the water cold.
