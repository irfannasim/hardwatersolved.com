The **best manganese filters for well water** are chosen by pH before they are chosen by ppm rating. Manganese needs a higher pH to oxidize than iron does. At pH 8.5 or above, or where the pH can be raised to it, the WECO KL-1054 Katalox Light filter is the strongest pick. Between about 6.8 and 7.5, the AFWFilters Filox 15 manganese dioxide filter is the practical one. Below 6.8, no filter here removes manganese reliably until the pH is corrected first.

The sellers' headline numbers, from 2 to 11 ppm of manganese, almost never say what pH they assume. The chart below fills that gap from maker and extension figures.

## Why pH Decides Which Manganese Filter Works

![Chart of the pH at which each manganese filter pick works: softener above 6.7, greensand 6.8, Birm 7.5, Katalox Light accepted from 5.8 but recommended at 8.5, chlorine fastest at 8.5, with no pH published for Filox or the Oceanic AIO](/diagrams/manganese-filter-picks-working-ph-range-chart.svg "The ppm rating means little until you know which pH it assumes")

Iron oxidizes readily at ordinary well pH. Manganese resists, and every oxidizing method needs the same or a higher pH to remove it. The figures we could confirm:

| Media or method | pH guidance for manganese | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Softening resin | pH above 6.7 (Penn State's condition for iron softening; manganese behaves alike) | Penn State Extension |
| Manganese greensand | poor below pH 6.8 | NDSU Extension |
| Birm | at least pH 7.5 for manganese (6.8 for iron) | Penn State Extension |
| Katalox Light | inlet pH 5.8 to 10.5 accepted; 8.5 recommended for manganese | Watch Water, the media maker |
| Chlorine oxidation | fastest at about pH 8.5 (8.0 for iron) | NDSU Extension |
| Filox (manganese dioxide) | no figure on the seller's pages we checked | AFWFilters |

Watch Water's [Katalox Light page](https://www.watchwater.de/katalox-light/) is unusually direct. Its capacity figures are "based on raw water pH 7.5 for iron removal and 8.5 for manganese removal," and below those levels it recommends raising the pH with sodium hydroxide. It also says to "maintain a feed-water pH of around 8.5 to ensure reliable manganese oxidation." The media accepts water from pH 5.8, so it will run at 7. It just will not remove manganese as well there.

That gives three groups of wells:

| Your raw water pH | What it means for a manganese filter |
|---|---|
| Below about 6.8 | Correct pH first. Most oxidizing media struggle with manganese, and a softener is outside Penn State's conditions |
| About 6.8 to 7.5 | Greensand-type and manganese dioxide media; a softener for low, dissolved manganese. Birm is not suitable yet |
| About 7.5 and above | All the media listed work; Katalox Light reaches its recommended manganese conditions at 8.5 |

## Pairing a Filter With pH Correction

On a well below about 6.8, the filter choice depends on the pH correction you add, so plan both together.

- **A calcite neutralizer** dissolves calcium into the water as it raises pH. It usually brings acidic water up to around neutral, which suits greensand-type and manganese dioxide media. It also adds hardness, so the softener after it must be sized for the extra.
- **Chemical feed** of soda ash or sodium hydroxide can push pH higher, toward the 8.5 Katalox Light's maker recommends for manganese. It is a metering pump and a chemical tank to keep stocked.
- **Plumbing order:** pH correction first, then the manganese filter, then the softener. The filter needs the corrected pH, and the softener needs the manganese gone.

If you have iron bacteria as well, a chlorine feed ahead of the filter handles both. Penn State notes, though, that chlorine is "not recommended" for very high manganese, because a very high pH is needed to oxidize it completely.

## Health Levels Change How You Verify

Manganese is more than a staining problem. [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/iron-and-manganese-in-private-water-systems) cites a recommended standard of less than 0.05 mg/L for staining and taste, and EPA's health advisory of 0.3 mg/L because of neurological effects from regular drinking above that level.

- **Under 0.3 mg/L,** the goal is stopping the black stains, and any pick here that suits your pH will do.
- **Above 0.3 mg/L,** or above 0.1 mg/L with an infant on the water, test the treated water at the tap within a month of installation and after any change in the well.

How the well test should be ordered, including total versus dissolved results, is set out in [water softener for well water](/blog/water-softener-for-well-water/).

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Manganese at or below 0.05 mg/L.** The black specks are probably coming from the plumbing or the softener itself, not the well.
- **pH below about 6.8 with no plan to correct it.** Buy the pH correction first, then retest manganese.
- **Iron bacteria slime in the toilet tank.** Disinfection comes before any filter, or the media fouls.
- **A low-yield well.** Every backwashing pick here needs about 5.5 to 10 gpm of backwash. If the pump cannot supply it, ask about a smaller tank or a different method before buying.
- **Drinking water is the only concern.** A point-of-use reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen tap is cheaper than a whole-house filter.

Test pH alongside manganese, then buy the media that works at that pH. For a high-pH well, the WECO KL-1054. For pH 6.8 to 7.5, the Filox 15. For trace, dissolved manganese above pH 6.7, a fine-mesh softener. And on an acidic well, correct the pH before buying any of them.
