**The Fleck 5600SXT is one of the best-supported softener control valves you can buy, but "Fleck 5600SXT" on a listing tells you about the valve, not the softener.** Pentair makes the valve. A seller adds the resin, the mineral tank, the brine tank, the bypass and the warranty. Two "48,000-grain Fleck 5600SXT" softeners can ship with different resin grades, different brine tanks and different support. Buy the valve for its parts availability and simple programming. Choose the seller on resin grade, tank size and warranty.

This Fleck 5600SXT review splits those two questions. The valve is rated from Pentair's service manual and technical sheet. The systems are compared from what each seller's own listing says ships in the box. Nothing here comes from physical testing.

## Fleck 5600SXT Review: The Valve on Its Own

![Diagram of a Fleck 5600SXT water softener showing the Pentair valve, controller and meter that are identical in every listing, and the resin, tanks, bypass, valve set-up and warranty each seller chooses](/diagrams/fleck-5600sxt-valve-vs-seller-supplied-parts.svg "Three parts come from Pentair; everything in a dashed box is the seller's choice")

The 5600SXT is a 3/4-inch piston valve with an electronic SXT controller. In a softener, it is almost always sold in its metered form, with a 3/4-inch turbine meter in the outlet. What Pentair's documents say about it:

| Feature | What Pentair's documents say |
|---|---|
| Regeneration modes | Four: meter immediate, meter delayed, time clock delayed, day of week |
| Reserve | Fixed gallons or a percentage "safety factor" from 0 to 50% |
| Day override | Forces a regeneration after a set number of days, or off |
| Power failure | Keeps time for at least 8 hours; settings stored in non-volatile memory |
| Mid-regeneration power cut | Saves its position and resumes when power returns |
| Operating pressure | Minimum 20 psi for regeneration; maximum 125 psi |
| Water temperature | Maximum 110°F |
| Flow, valve alone | About 19 gpm at a 1 bar (about 15 psi) drop; about 25 gpm at 1.8 bar |
| Tank fit | Injector and flow controls sized per tank diameter, 6 to 14 inches listed |

### What the valve does well

**Parts are everywhere.** The 5600 has been in production for decades, and every specialist supplier sells its seal and spacer kits, pistons, injectors, meters and SXT boards. A seal kit is a small expense next to a new valve. That is the single biggest reason to choose it. How these parts wear and how a rebuild is done is covered in [servicing a water softener control valve](/blog/servicing-a-water-softener-control-valve/).

**Programming is fully exposed.** Capacity, hardness, reserve, day override, regeneration time and each cycle's length are all set from the front keypad. That matters, because the factory capacity setting is usually the box rating and needs lowering for an efficient salt dose. How the valve uses those numbers is explained in [how a water softener control valve works](/blog/how-a-water-softener-control-valve-works/).

**It is a known quantity to plumbers.** Fleck valves are familiar to most water treatment technicians, so you are not tied to the seller for service.

### Where the valve is limited

**It is a 3/4-inch valve.** Pentair's sheet gives the valve alone about 19 gpm at a 15 psi drop. A full softener flows less, because the resin bed adds resistance. That is ample for most homes, but large houses with many bathrooms are better served by a larger valve such as the Fleck 7000SXT.

**Some listings stretch the flow numbers.** One 64,000-grain listing claims "up to 16 gpm"; others have quoted peak flows as high as 28 gpm. Pentair's sheet puts the valve alone at about 25 gpm at a 26 psi drop. Any system figure above that is not credible, and a system figure near it means a pressure drop you will notice in the shower.

**The display and keypad are basic.** No app, no Wi-Fi, no salt sensor. For many owners that is an advantage.

What the table shows:

1. **The resin grade is the biggest real difference, and the one most often missing.** On chlorinated city water, 10% crosslink resin lasts noticeably longer. On AFWFilters' own store, the upgrade from 8% to 10% on a 48,000-grain system adds only a little to the price. If a listing does not state the grade, assume standard. The trade-off is set out in [standard vs chlorine-resistant softener resin](/blog/standard-vs-chlorine-resistant-softener-resin/).
2. **Tank geometry changes the set-up.** The 64,000-grain listing uses a 12-inch tank. Pentair's manual specifies a different injector for 12-inch tanks than for 10-inch ones. A reputable seller ships the valve configured for the tank. Check the injector colour against the manual if you are ever unsure.
3. **Brine tank size changes how often you add salt.** An 18 x 33 round tank holds more salt than a 14 x 36, which matters on hard water.
4. **The warranty is the seller's, not Pentair's.** Who honours it, and how quickly, is part of what you are buying.

## Capacity at a Stated Salt Dose

Every listing names its softener by grain rating. Those ratings are the maximum-salt figure, and the usable figure is lower. At an efficient 6 lb of salt per cubic foot of resin, standard resin delivers roughly 21,000 grains per cubic foot. Applied to these systems:

| Resin volume | Sold as | At 6 lb/cu ft | At 15 lb/cu ft | Salt per regeneration at 6 lb/cu ft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 cu ft | 48,000 grain | about 31,500 | about 46,500 | 9 lb |
| 2.0 cu ft | 64,000 grain | about 42,000 | about 62,000 | 12 lb |

The method and the full table are in [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/). The practical step is the same on every 5600SXT: set the controller's unit capacity to the usable figure for the salt dose you choose, not to the box number. Because the 5600SXT exposes that setting on its keypad, this takes two minutes.

## Programming a 5600SXT System: The Numbers That Matter

Most sellers ship the controller pre-set. Check every one of these values against your own water, because the seller's defaults are a guess about your house. Here is a worked example for a 1.5 cu ft, 48,000-grain system serving four people on 15 gpg water, about 300 gallons and 4,500 grains of hardness a day.

| Setting (manual's display code) | What it does | Example value | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regeneration type | Meter delayed is the usual home setting | Meter delayed | Regenerates at night once capacity is used |
| Unit capacity (C) | Grains the bed delivers per regeneration | 31,500 | Usable figure at 6 lb/cu ft, not the 48,000 box rating |
| Feed water hardness (H) | Your hardness, plus about 4 gpg per ppm of iron | 15 | From your water test |
| Reserve (RC or SF) | Soft water held back for the last day | 300 gallons, or about 20% | One day of use; the manual caps it at half the system capacity |
| Day override (DO) | Forces a regeneration after this many days | Set, not off | So a lightly used softener still regenerates |
| Regeneration time (RT) | When delayed regenerations start | 2:00 a.m. | When no one is using water |
| Brine refill time (BF) | Sets the salt dose | Depends on the brine refill control | See below |

The controller works out the gallons between regenerations from capacity divided by hardness, then subtracts the reserve. With the example values, that is 2,100 gallons of capacity and about 1,800 gallons before a regeneration is triggered, roughly six days for this household.

**Salt dose comes from the refill time.** The 5600SXT measures salt by how long it refills the brine tank. Pentair's manual rates its 0.25 gpm brine refill control at 0.75 lb of salt per minute. If your valve has that control, 9 lb of salt (6 lb per cubic foot on 1.5 cu ft) needs a 12-minute refill. A 0.50 gpm control delivers 1.5 lb per minute, so the same dose takes 6 minutes. Ask the seller which control is fitted, and set the refill time and capacity to match each other.

Get these two numbers to agree and the 5600SXT runs efficiently. Leave the capacity at the box rating with an efficient refill, and it will run out of soft water before it regenerates. The reasoning behind the reserve setting is in [how to set softener capacity and reserve](/blog/how-to-set-softener-capacity-and-reserve/).

## Choosing a 5600SXT System for Your Water

| Your water | Pick |
|---|---|
| City water, chlorinated | 1.5 cu ft with 10% crosslink resin (like the AFWFilters 48k 10% resin system) |
| City water, chloramine | 10% resin at minimum; consider a carbon stage ahead |
| Well water, hardness only | Standard 1.5 or 2.0 cu ft system |
| Well water with low dissolved iron | A fine-mesh resin version of the same valve; standard resin is not the right choice |
| Large household, very hard water | 2.0 cu ft with an 18 x 33 brine tank; check flow needs against the 3/4-inch valve |

## Who Should Not Buy a Fleck 5600SXT Softener

- **Very large homes with high simultaneous demand.** A 3/4-inch valve is the bottleneck. Look at a larger valve.
- **Anyone who wants app control, salt monitoring or alerts.** The SXT controller has none of these.
- **Buyers who will not check the listing details.** If you buy on the valve name alone, you may get standard resin on chlorinated water or an undersized brine tank.
- **Wells with significant iron, manganese or sulfur.** The valve is fine, but a softener alone is not the answer. Treat those first.
- **Anyone who will not reprogram the capacity.** Left at the box rating with an efficient salt dose, any 5600SXT system can run out of soft water before it regenerates.

The verdict of this Fleck 5600SXT review: the valve is the safe choice, cheap to maintain and serviceable by almost anyone. The softener is only as good as the resin, tank and warranty the seller pairs with it, so compare those, set the capacity honestly, and the 5600SXT will do its job for a long time.
