The **best water softener replacement valves** for most homes are metered Fleck heads: the **Fleck 5600SXT** for tanks from 8 to 12 inches across, and the **Fleck 5800SXT** for 13 to 16 inch tanks or higher flows. A metered 5600SXT head costs well under a complete new softener from the same seller. But a head only works on an old tank if six things around it fit, and most failed swaps go wrong on those, not on the valve itself.

So this page starts with the fit checklist, then matches each replacement head to a tank size.

## First, Is It the Valve?

A softener that has stopped softening often has nothing wrong with its valve head. A blocked injector, an air leak in the brine line or a salt bridge produces the same hard water, and the fixes cost a few dollars. Rule those out with [why a water softener is not using salt](/blog/water-softener-not-using-salt/) before you buy a head.

If the valve really is at fault, try the cheap repair next. Worn seals, spacers and pistons cause most internal leaks and drains that never stop, and [servicing a water softener control valve](/blog/servicing-a-water-softener-control-valve/) walks through the rebuild for Fleck, Clack and Autotrol. A whole new head is the answer when the body is cracked, the circuit board is obsolete, parts are no longer made, or you want to upgrade an old timer valve to a metered one.

## The Fit Checklist: Six Measurements Before You Buy

![Water softener replacement valve fit checklist: six measurement points on the tank top, from the yoke and drain port to the 2-1/2 inch neck thread, 1.050 inch riser and tank diameter](/diagrams/softener-replacement-valve-fit-measurement-points.svg "The head is the easy part. The thread, riser, flow control and bypass decide whether it fits")

Work through these with the old valve still on the tank where you can, and write each answer down.

1. **Tank neck thread.** Almost every residential softener tank has a 2-1/2 inch opening with 8 threads per inch. Pentair's [whole-house softener spec sheet](https://www.pentair.com/content/dam/extranet/web/nam/pentair/spec-sheets/english/4005646-rotary-valve-water-softener-system-spec-sheet.pdf) gives that thread for its 10x44, 10x54 and 12x48 tanks, which is why heads from different brands fit the same tanks. A tank with a 4 inch opening needs a commercial valve, and none of the picks below.
2. **Riser tube diameter.** The tube up the middle of the tank, also called the distributor tube, is 1.050 inches across the outside on common home valves. That is 3/4 inch pipe size. Measure it with calipers or a tape. A 1.32 inch (1 inch pipe) tube means a larger valve family.
3. **Riser height.** Each valve sets how far the riser must sit relative to the tank lip, and Fleck's and Clack's figures are not interchangeable. A tube cut for one valve may need trimming, or replacing, for another. Check the new head's manual before you cut.
4. **Tank diameter.** It decides the backwash rate, which is set by a small washer in the drain fitting called the drain line flow control. For standard resin, the commonly used figures are 1.5 to 2.0 gpm for an 8 inch tank, 2.4 to 3.5 gpm for 10 inch and 3.5 to 5.0 gpm for 12 inch. The new head must ship with, or accept, the right one. Clack's [WS1 service manual](https://www.clackcorp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/V3115-99-WS1-1.25-DRAWINGS-AND-SERVICE-MANUAL.pdf) lists drain flow controls from 0.7 to 10 gpm for its 3/4 inch drain fitting, which shows how wide that choice is.
5. **The brine side.** Most homes run a 3/8 inch brine line to a safety float in the brine tank. Fleck heads pair with Fleck's 2310 safety float and an air check. Some valves refill by time, others with softened water, and the float must suit the method.
6. **Bypass or yoke.** Fleck heads connect through a Fleck yoke or bypass, in 3/4 or 1 inch. A Clack WS1 uses its own bypass. Changing valve brand almost always means changing the bypass.

Also check the obvious: a power outlet within reach of the transformer cord, and which side of the old valve the supply enters, so the new head and its meter are plumbed the right way round.

## What a Valve Swap Costs Against a New Softener

The options below run from least to most expensive, before labour. Prices change often, so check the current price of each before deciding.

| Option | Parts |
|---|---|
| Seal and spacer rebuild (Fleck 5800 piston, seals and spacers) | rebuild kit |
| New 5600SXT head on your tank, old Fleck bypass reused | head |
| New 5600SXT head with a new 1 inch Fleck bypass | head plus bypass |
| New 5600SXT head, new bypass and a 1.5 cu ft 10% rebed | head, bypass, resin kit |
| Complete new 48,000-grain Fleck 5600SXT softener, 10% resin | whole system |

The head on its own is the clear saving. Once the resin also needs replacing, the gap to a whole new softener becomes small, and the new unit brings a new tank and brine tank as well. ## Installing and Setting Up the New Head

![Grey gate valve on a black water main pipe. Close the main supply shutoff before removing an old softener head and fitting a replacement](https://images.pexels.com/photos/13726337/pexels-photo-13726337.jpeg)

1. **Shut the water off and bypass the softener.** Then relieve pressure by starting a manual regeneration or opening a downstream tap.
2. **Unscrew the old head.** It can be stiff. Lift it straight off so the riser tube stays in place, and check the top basket and the O-ring at the riser.
3. **Inspect the riser and resin.** Resin beads at your taps before the swap mean a cracked riser or distributor. Replace it now, while the head is off.
4. **Lubricate only with silicone grease.** Petroleum grease swells valve seals. Use it on the tank O-ring and riser O-ring, as the manual directs.
5. **Fit the flow control, bypass and brine line.** Confirm the drain line flow control rating against your tank diameter, and that the brine line and float suit the new valve. Make any solder joints away from the valve, and keep at least 6 inches between solder joints and the drain fitting, as Fleck and Clack both require.
6. **Program it.** Set the time, your hardness, the capacity for your actual resin volume at your chosen salt dose, and the regeneration time. Then run a manual regeneration and watch each stage.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Cabinet softeners with a built-in valve.** Many all-in-one units sold at home centres pair a proprietary valve with their own tank and brine cabinet. A standard head is not a drop-in replacement there.
- **Dealer-installed systems under warranty or service contract.** Fitting a different head may void what you are paying for.
- **Owners whose valve only leaks internally.** A seal and spacer kit usually fixes it for a fraction of the price.
- **Systems where the tank is cracked or the resin is worn out and the valve is old.** At that point, new parts on an old base cost nearly as much as a new softener.

For most owners with a sound 8 to 12 inch tank, the answer is a metered Fleck 5600SXT head, the right drain flow control and, if the brand changes, a matching bypass. Measure the six points first, and the swap is a short job.
