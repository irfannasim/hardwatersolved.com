## Before You Buy: Does the Tank Need Replacing?

Most brine tank complaints are not tank faults. An overflowing tank is a valve or float problem. A tank with a hard crust or a hollow bridge of salt needs breaking up and cleaning, not replacing. A softener that has stopped using salt usually has a blocked injector or brine line, covered in [why a water softener is not using salt](/blog/water-softener-not-using-salt/).

Replace the tank when it is **cracked, split at the overflow fitting, warped, or too small** for how often you are refilling it. If only the float or brine well has failed, the part costs far less than the tank: AFWFilters lists a Fleck 2310 safety float valve at about $29, a complete float and air check assembly at about $79, and a 4 inch brine well at about $45. How each part works and how to test it is in [water softener brine tank internals](/blog/water-softener-brine-tank-internals/).

## Size vs Salt Capacity vs Refill Interval

The salt a tank holds sets how often you lift a bag. Sellers state the capacity for each size. The interval depends on how much salt your softener uses, which is set by your hardness, your water use and the salt dose programmed into the valve.

**Estimating your salt use.** A metered softener at an efficient 6 lb per cubic foot salt setting gets about 3,500 grains of hardness removal per pound of salt, using published design guidance for standard resin of about 21,000 grains per cubic foot at that dose, summarised in [*Water Conditioning & Purification*](https://wcponline.com/2004/03/14/learn-right-first-time-guidelines-designing-water-softeners/). A family of four using about 260 gallons a day on 15 gpg water removes about 3,900 grains a day, so it uses a little over a pound of salt a day. That is about 35 to 40 lb a month. Timer valves and heavy salt settings use far more.

## The Parts That Must Match Your Valve

A replacement tank arrives with parts already fitted. Check each one against what came out of the old tank before you order.

| Part | What must match | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety float and brine valve** | The valve family. Fleck valves pair with Fleck's 2310 safety float. AFWFilters sells its float-only tank versions for Autotrol valves and its float plus air check versions for Fleck | Read the part number on the old float body |
| **Air check** | Fitted at the bottom of the brine assembly on valves that draw brine through it, to stop air reaching the resin tank | Look for a check ball housing at the foot of the old float rod |
| **Brine line size** | 3/8 inch tubing on most home softeners | Measure the old tube's outside diameter |
| **Brine well diameter** | 4 inch wells are common; the float must slide freely inside | Measure the old well |
| **Float height** | On metered valves the valve times the refill and the float is only a backstop. On float-controlled valves the float height sets the salt dose | Note the old clip position before you take it apart |
| **Overflow fitting** | Must sit below the float's shut-off level, with its own gravity line to a drain | Check the new tank's elbow height |
| **Salt grid** | Keep one if the old tank had one; Fleck's manual says to cut the air check level with the grid | Look under the old salt |

The two that cause most trouble are the float and the overflow. A float meant for one valve can shut off too early, starving the refill, or never close at all. And an overflow line teed into the softener's pressurised drain line can push water back into the tank. The right overflow route is set out in [brine tank full of water or overflowing](/blog/brine-tank-full-of-water-or-overflowing/).

## Swapping the Tank

1. **Bypass the softener** and unplug the valve, or set it so it will not regenerate during the job.
2. **Disconnect the brine line** at the float assembly, and the overflow tubing at the elbow.
3. **Empty and lift out the old tank.** Scoop out remaining salt and water first. A full tank is far too heavy to move.
4. **Set the new tank on a level, clean floor** close enough that the brine line runs without kinks.
5. **Reconnect the brine line** to the new float assembly, and run the overflow to a floor drain or standpipe by gravity, on its own line.
6. **Add water and salt.** Add a few gallons of water first, or start a manual refill, then fill with salt, keeping the salt level above the water.
7. **Run a manual regeneration** and watch the brine draw lower the water level. The float should not interfere with a normal refill.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Owners of cabinet softeners.** The brine tank is part of the housing.
- **Anyone whose tank overflows.** Fix the valve, float or injector. A new tank will overflow the same way.
- **Tanks with only a failed float or brine well.** Replace the part for a fraction of the price.
- **Salt bridges and mushing.** Break up and clean out the old salt, then change salt type if needed. The tank is not the problem.

For most homes, the answer is an 18x33 round tank with a safety float and air check that match the valve. Choose the 18x40 if you want fewer top-ups, and a square tank only where an 18 inch circle will not fit.
