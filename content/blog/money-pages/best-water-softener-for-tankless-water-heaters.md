The **best water softener for tankless water heaters** is any properly sized salt-based softener that brings your water under the heater maker's warranty limit. That limit is about 12 grains per gallon (200 mg/L) for Navien, Noritz, Rinnai and Rheem, and 7 grains for Takagi. Our picks are the **SoftPro Elite 48k** if you want the longest valve warranty and upflow efficiency, and the **AFWFilters Fleck 5600SXT 48k with 10% resin** for the same job at about half the price. The **Express Water WH100SP** polyphosphate system is only for water that is already under the limit. Start with the heater's paperwork, because the manufacturer's clause sets the spec.

## What Five Tankless Makers Actually Require

Each line below is quoted from a current manufacturer document, checked on 1 October 2026. Wording changes between models and editions, so the manual and warranty that came with your heater are the ones that count.

| Maker | Document checked | Hardness limit | Treatment wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rinnai** | [N-Series residential condensing installation and operation manual](https://media.rinnai.us/salsify_asset/s-b705c649-d604-4613-9696-374240bbf008/100000467-N%20Series%20Residential%20Condensing%20Installation%20and%20Operation%20Manual.pdf) | "Total Hardness Up to 200 mg/L" | Where water is hard, "the water must be treated and may require a more frequent flushing schedule". "Damage caused by lime build-up is not covered by the water heater's warranty" |
| **Navien** | NPE condensing water heater installation manual | "Total Hardness Up to 200 mg/l (12 grains/gallon)" | "Damage caused by poor water quality is not covered under warranty" |
| **Rheem** | [RTGH condensing tankless use and care manual](https://files.myrheem.com/webpartners/ProductDocuments/43BB500D-4CDB-4512-B3A3-1E1F15C0EC2F.pdf) and residential tankless warranty (AP21265 Rev 04) | "Total Hardness Up to 200 mg/L" | Outside the table, Rheem recommends you "install a water treatment device or water softener at the same time as the original installation". The warranty excludes failures "caused by lime, mineral build-up, or scale" |
| **Noritz** | Online limited warranty | "hardness levels in excess of 12 grains per gallon (200 mg/L)" excluded | Also excludes "untreated or poorly treated well water" and high pH |
| **Takagi** | [Residential limited gas tankless warranty](https://www.takagi.com/media/31647/100287499.pdf) (100287499 Rev C) | "more than 7 grains per gallon (120 ppm) for single family domestic applications" excluded | Also excludes "pool water, spa water, or any chemically treated water" and "untreated or poorly treated well water" |

Three things follow from the table:

1. **The limit is a number on a hardness test.** Every clause is written as a measured hardness, not as "scale". A treatment that prevents scale but leaves the test reading unchanged does not move you under the line.
2. **Takagi's line is the strictest.** At 7 grains, a lot of water that is merely "moderately hard" is already outside Takagi's residential cover.
3. **Rheem names the softener outright.** Rinnai says "treated". Rheem says "water treatment device or water softener", and asks for it "at the same time as the original installation".

Navien also caps iron at 0.3 mg/L, manganese at 0.05 mg/L, pH at 6.5 to 8.5 and total dissolved solids at 500 mg/L, and Rinnai and Rheem list the same ceilings. A softener does not change pH or TDS. Well owners should test every row, not only hardness. The site's guide to [softened water and tankless water heaters](/blog/softened-water-and-tankless-water-heaters/) explains how softening changes flushing and warranty standing.

## Size the Softener for Flow as Well as Grains

A tank-type water heater buffers demand. A tankless one does not. Every gallon of hot water passes through the softener first, at the moment you use it. Rinnai's condensing manual lists maximum flows of 7 to 11 gpm across its models. A softener whose service flow is well below your heater's working flow will cut pressure at the shower when two fixtures run together.

1. **Find the heater's maximum flow** in its manual, then think about the busiest real moment: two showers and a kitchen tap, for example.
2. **Compare it with the softener's service flow**, not just its peak figure. Peak flows are for short bursts.
3. **Size grain capacity from your household's daily hardness load.** The method, including a day of reserve, is in [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/).
4. **Plumb the softener ahead of the heater**, with the heater's isolation valves kept in place for flushing. Rinnai "strongly recommends" isolation valves, and Rheem sells them as an accessory.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Owners whose water already tests under 7 gpg.** You are under every limit in the table. Flush on the manual's schedule and keep the test result on file.
- **Wells with iron, manganese or low pH outside the table.** A softener fixes one row. Iron above 0.3 mg/L or pH below 6.5 needs its own treatment first.
- **Anyone who will not keep the softener in salt.** A softener left empty or in bypass puts the heater straight back on hard water, and the warranty clause does not care why. [Scale in a tankless water heater](/blog/scale-in-tankless-water-heater/) shows how quickly it builds.
- **Homes where the heater is the only concern and the water is just over the line**, around 12 to 14 gpg. A softener is still the warranty-safe answer. If you choose flushing alone, though, know that it does not change the test result.

Read your heater's water-quality table first, test your water against it, and buy the treatment that moves the measured number under the line. For most tankless homes above 12 grains, that is a metered, salt-based softener sized for flow.
