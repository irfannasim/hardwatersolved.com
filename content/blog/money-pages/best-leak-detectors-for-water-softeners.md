The best **leak detectors for water softeners** are automatic shutoffs, not alarm-only sensors. For most homes that means a **Moen Flo** cut into the main, which watches flow and pressure for the whole house and runs a daily leak test, or a **Guardian by Elexa** kit, which clamps onto the ball valve you already have and closes it when a floor sensor gets wet. Where you put the sensors matters as much as which kit you buy. The low point under the softener valve, the low side of the brine tank and the water heater pan come first.

A softener has a supply connection, a bypass, a pressurised drain line, a brine line and an overflow, and it usually regenerates at 2 a.m. That is the case for something that closes the water, not just beeps.

## What Actually Leaks on a Softener

Leak protection is only as good as its match to the failure. These are the softener leaks it has to catch, and what each one looks like.

| Leak | How it starts | How fast | What catches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burst supply connector or cracked bypass | Pressure surge, old braided hose, freeze | Full main flow in seconds | Any automatic shutoff; a floor sensor below the valve |
| Drain line blown out of the standpipe | Regeneration pushes water under pressure | A few gallons a minute, for an hour or more, at night | Floor sensor near the softener; flow monitoring |
| Brine tank overflow | Valve refilling too long, stuck float | Slow, steady, at night | Floor sensor on the low side of the brine tank |
| Weep at the tank base, valve or fittings | Old O-rings, cracked fitting | Drops a minute, for weeks | Daily leak test on a flow-monitoring shutoff, or a sensor right below it |

The slow ones do the expensive damage. Washington State's insurance regulator notes in its guide to [leaks, water damage and mold](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/home-insurance/how-home-insurance-works/leaks-water-damage-and-mold) that home insurance "usually covers sudden leaks, but may not cover gradual leaks." A detector that turns a months-long weep into a same-day alert is protecting your claim as well as your floor. How those claims are judged is covered in [homeowners insurance and water softener leaks](/blog/homeowners-insurance-water-softener-leak/), and the drain line's own failure points are in [water softener drain line leaking](/blog/water-softener-drain-line-leaking/).

## Alarm-Only Sensors vs Automatic Shutoffs

| Type | What it does | Catches | Misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alarm-only floor sensor** | Beeps or sends an app alert when wet | Water that reaches the sensor | Everything if nobody hears it; water keeps running |
| **Sensor-triggered shutoff** (Guardian by Elexa) | Closes your existing main valve when a sensor gets wet | Water that reaches a sensor | Leaks away from any sensor, such as inside a wall |
| **Flow-monitoring shutoff** (Moen Flo, Phyn) | Watches flow and pressure on the main; closes on abnormal flow; runs a leak test | Any leak after the valve, including drips and hidden ones | Needs power and Wi-Fi for full function; learns your patterns first |

An alarm is the right add-on, not the whole answer. If the softener drain line blows out at 2 a.m. while you sleep two floors up, or while you are away, an alarm only tells you about the water afterwards. A shutoff stops it.

## Where to Put Leak Detectors Around a Softener

![Leak detector placement plan around a water softener and water heater: auto shutoff on the main, four floor sensor spots, and no sensor at the floor drain](/diagrams/softener-utility-room-leak-detector-placement-plan.svg "Shutoff upstream of the softener, sensors at the low points, none where regeneration water splashes")

1. **Put the shutoff on the main, upstream of the softener's bypass.** Closing it then isolates the softener, the water heater and everything after them. A shutoff downstream of the softener protects nothing at the softener.
2. **Sensor 1: the floor below the softener's valve and bypass.** This is where a cracked fitting or a weeping O-ring drips first. Find the low point of the floor, which is rarely the middle.
3. **Sensor 2: beside the brine tank, on the low side.** A brine tank that overflows, or a split brine line, puts water here before anywhere else.
4. **Sensor 3: in the water heater's drain pan.** The water heater is the other big leak in most utility rooms, and the pan collects it.
5. **Sensor 4: under the main shutoff and meter.** Supply-side leaks before the shutoff are the one leak an automatic valve cannot stop, so you want to know fast.
6. **Not at the floor drain or standpipe.** The softener sends regeneration water there every few nights. A sensor where that splashes will raise false alarms until you switch it off.

## Living With a Smart Shutoff and a Softener

- **Note the regeneration time.** Metered softeners usually regenerate in the early hours, and a cycle sends water to the drain for an hour or more. A flow alert at that time is usually the softener. Penn State Extension puts a typical regeneration at about 50 gallons.
- **Keep the leak test and regeneration apart.** If your shutoff runs a nightly test, set the softener's regeneration to a different hour so the test is not upset by drain flow.
- **Test the sensors twice a year.** Touch each one with a damp cloth and confirm the valve closes.
- **Keep the overflow and drain lines visible.** A sensor cannot help with water it never reaches.

## Matching the Shutoff to Your Main

Check three things at the main before you order:

| Check | Moen Flo | Phyn (Protect / Plus 2nd Gen) | Guardian by Elexa |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pipe size** | Buy the 3/4 or 1 inch version to match the main | Mains 1-1/4 inch or smaller; 1 inch NPSM ends, adapters sold separately | Clamps on ball valves from 1/2 to 1-1/4 inch, per Guardian |
| **Existing valve** | Not needed; the Flo is its own valve | Not needed; Phyn is its own valve | Needs a working quarter-turn ball valve |
| **Power and signal** | Outlet within 10 feet, or Moen's 25-foot extension | Outlet within 12 feet, Wi-Fi at the install point | Powered valve controller with an optional battery backup, per Guardian |

Size an inline shutoff to the pipe, never smaller, so it adds little pressure loss at normal household flow. If the main is an old gate valve, a plumber fitting a Flo or Phyn can replace it in the same visit, which is the cheaper route than adding a ball valve just for Guardian.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Renters who cannot alter the plumbing.** A battery alarm sensor beside the brine tank is the practical step.
- **Homes on a well with a gate valve on the main.** Guardian needs a quarter-turn ball valve, and a flow monitor may need a plumber to change the valve first. Price both before deciding.
- **Softeners in a garage or outbuilding with a floor drain and nothing to damage.** The case for a shutoff weakens when a leak simply drains away.
- **Owners hoping a detector fixes a leak.** If the softener already leaks, fix the fitting or valve first. A brine tank whose water level climbs every cycle is usually a softener that has stopped drawing brine, and the causes are in [why a water softener is not using salt](/blog/water-softener-not-using-salt/).

For most homes, the strongest setup is an automatic shutoff on the main ahead of the softener, plus floor sensors at the low points around the softener, brine tank and water heater. A Moen Flo covers the whole house. A Guardian kit gets most of the protection without cutting a pipe.
