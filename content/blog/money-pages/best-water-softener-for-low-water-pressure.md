Three things come out of it.

- **The cabinet units that do publish a system figure lose a lot at a real peak.** Whirlpool's own manual puts the WHES30 at 15 psi at just 7.6 gpm. On a house that starts at 40 psi, that unit at 12 gpm would leave a trickle.
- **The Fleck 5600SXT and Aquatrol figures are for the valve alone.** Pentair's [Fleck 5600SXT specification sheet](https://goodwaterwarehouse.com/manuals-specs-brochures/5600sxt-spec-sheet-42749.pdf) gives 20 gpm at a 15 psi drop and 26 gpm at 25 psi, with a Cv of 5.0. The resin tank and bed add more, so the whole system loses more than 5.4 psi. Sellers quote about 12 gpm of service flow for a 48k 5600SXT system.
- **Some makers publish no drop at all.** SoftPro quotes a flow rate; AFWFilters quotes service and peak flow for the twin. Neither gives the pressure lost at that flow. Ask before you buy. NSF/ANSI 44 limits a certified softener's drop to 15 psi at its rated service flow, so a certified unit's service flow is at least a ceiling.

## Minimum Inlet Pressure: The Units That Will Not Work

![Range bars of water softener minimum inlet pressure against well switch cut-ins: Aquasure Harmony 25 psi minimum, Fleck 5600SXT, SoftPro Elite and Whirlpool 20 psi, with 20/40, 30/50 and 40/60 cut-in lines](/diagrams/low-water-pressure-softener-minimum-inlet-vs-well-cut-in.svg "On a 20/40 switch, the pressure falls below some valves' minimum every pump cycle")

Below its minimum inlet pressure, a softener's injector may not draw brine, so it stops softening while water still flows. The published minimums:

- **Fleck 5600SXT:** 20 to 125 psi working pressure (Pentair).
- **SoftPro Elite:** 20 to 125 psi (SoftPro's warranty conditions).
- **Aquasure Harmony:** 25 psi minimum, 125 psi maximum (Aquasure's manual). The valve's own spec table lists 20 to 125 psi, but the manual's installation conditions, which the warranty depends on, say 25.

On a well, the lowest pressure the softener sees is the pressure switch **cut-in**, the point where the pump restarts. With a 20/40 switch the pressure falls to 20 psi on every pump cycle: below Aquasure's minimum and right at the edge for the others. A 30/50 or 40/60 switch keeps every valve here in range. How to check your own numbers is in [water softener pressure requirements](/blog/water-softener-pressure-requirements/).

## Measure Before You Buy

You need two readings, both at a hose bib or a gauge near the main:

1. **Static pressure,** with every tap closed. On a well, watch the gauge through a full pump cycle and note the lowest reading, the cut-in.
2. **Flowing pressure,** with two or three fixtures open at once. The difference between the two is what your pipes already lose.

Then budget the softener's loss. Subtract its drop at your peak from your lowest supply pressure. As a rule of thumb, aim for a softener that costs no more than about 8 to 10 psi at your realistic peak. If a softener is already installed and pressure fell afterwards, the causes are covered in [low water pressure after a water softener](/blog/low-water-pressure-after-water-softener/).

## Other Things That Cost Pressure Around a Softener

- **The bypass.** A partly closed bypass or a 3/4 in bypass on a 1 inch main costs pressure. Check it is fully in service.
- **Prefilters.** A sediment cartridge ahead of the softener adds its own loss, which grows as it loads. Change it on pressure drop, not on the calendar.
- **A fouled resin bed.** Iron, silt or broken-down resin raises the bed's resistance over the years. A softener that once flowed well and now does not may need its resin replaced, not a bigger unit.
- **Undersized tanks.** A small tank on a big flow pushes water through the bed fast. The resin also softens less completely at excessive flow.
- **Pipe size between meter and softener.** A long 3/4 in run to a 1 inch softener makes the softener's ports irrelevant.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Homes below 20 psi static.** No softener here is rated to work. Fix the pressure first.
- **Wells on a 20/40 switch you cannot change.** The pressure falls to the edge of every valve's minimum on each pump cycle.
- **Large homes with high peak demand on a weak supply.** Look for a 1.25 in high-flow valve such as a Fleck 7000SXT, and ask for its system pressure drop.
- **Anyone whose low pressure has not been diagnosed.** A failing pressure-reducing valve, a clogged main shutoff or a waterlogged pressure tank all look like "low pressure" and are cheaper to fix than any softener.

The best water softener for low water pressure is the one whose pressure loss you know before it is installed. Measure your lowest supply pressure, check each valve's minimum against it, and ask for the drop at your peak flow in writing.
