## What the Evidence Says, in One Paragraph

The strongest independent test of home descalers is [WateReuse Research Foundation project 08-06](https://watereuse.org/watereuse-research/08-06-evaluation-of-alternatives-to-domestic-ion-exchange-water-softeners/), led by Arizona State University. Using the German DVGW W 512 heated protocol, it found that "both electromagnetic treatment and electrically induced precipitation reduced scale formation by approximately 50 percent", and that the scale left behind was "a 'soft' scale that was easily removed by brushing". It found no change in hardness, which is why no descaler can fix spotting, soap scum or lather. The full picture, including where the wider research disagrees, is in our comparison of a [water softener vs an electronic descaler](/blog/water-softener-vs-electronic-descaler/). For this page, the takeaway is practical: a result is possible, not likely enough to buy on faith.

## The Kettle Scale Test to Run During the Refund Period

![Black electric kettle beside a kitchen sink and mixer tap, set up to boil the same cold tap water before and after an electronic descaler is fitted](https://images.pexels.com/photos/39338456/pexels-photo-39338456.jpeg)

A hardness test cannot tell you anything about a descaler, because the minerals stay in the water. Why that trips people up is covered in [scale prevention vs hardness removal](/blog/scale-prevention-vs-hardness-removal/). Scale is what you are paying to reduce, so measure scale. An electric kettle concentrates weeks of it in one visible spot.

![Timeline of electronic water descaler refund windows against a 42-day kettle scale test: ScaleBlaster's 90 days, Eddy's 12 months and HydroFLOW's warranty-only terms](/diagrams/electronic-descaler-refund-window-vs-kettle-test-timeline.svg "Finish the test by day 42, while every refund window is still open")

**You need:** one electric kettle used only for the test, citric acid or white vinegar to descale it, a phone camera and a notebook.

1. **Days 0 to 14, baseline.** Leave the descaler in its box. Descale the kettle until the base is bright. Every day, fill it to the same mark from the same cold tap and boil it the same number of times, for example three. Empty it after it cools. Do not rinse or wipe it. On day 14, photograph the base in the same light, from the same angle. Then wipe one half with a dry cloth and note whether the scale comes away or stays put.
2. **Days 14 to 28, fit and settle.** Fit the unit on the main line, upstream of the kitchen tap, following the maker's coil or clamp instructions. Descale the kettle again. Keep using the tap as normal. Makers describe changes building over weeks, so give it this period.
3. **Days 28 to 42, treated run.** Descale the kettle one more time and repeat exactly the step-1 routine for 14 days. Photograph it and do the same wipe test.
4. **Day 42, decide.** Compare the two photos and the two wipe tests. The independent study found treated scale was softer and easier to brush off. If the treated run looks the same as the baseline, and wipes off no more easily, ask for your refund while there is time left.

Keep the photos with your receipt. For HydroFLOW in particular, they are the evidence its warranty asks for. If the result is borderline and your window is a year long, repeat the treated run at around six months.

Two things this test cannot do: tell you about scale inside a water heater, which takes far longer to show, or tell you the water is softer. It is a quick, fair check of the one thing a descaler claims to change.

## Fitting One So the Test Is Fair

Where the unit goes decides whether the kettle ever sees treated water.

- **Put it upstream of the tap you test from.** A unit on the main line, after the meter or pressure-reducing valve and before the first branch, treats every tap. HydroFLOW's guide recommends the cold feed to the water heater "for optimal performance", but a kitchen cold tap is not downstream of that point. Because the Pearl Plus clamps on with cable ties and comes with a removal tool, fit it on the main line for the six-week test, then move it to the heater feed if you keep it.
- **Follow the coil or clamp count exactly.** ScaleBlaster's FAQ says to wrap its coil the recommended number of times and not to over-wrap. Eddy's coils are also wound around the pipe, to the count in its instructions. A short-cut installation is a fair reason for a seller to refuse a refund.
- **Check for an electrical loop on metal pipe.** HydroFLOW warns that a unit fitted where current has an easy path from one side to the other traps the signal, "which damages the equipment along with greatly reducing its performance". Its fix is to move the unit outside the loop, for example before the earth grounding point. Plastic pipe avoids the problem.
- **Plan the power.** All three need a nearby outlet. HydroFLOW's power supply is rated for 41 to 104°F, so an unheated garage in winter may be outside its tested range.
- **Expect your joints to need a look.** HydroFLOW's warranty excludes leaks caused when existing scale loosens, and says owners have a duty to inspect compression joints periodically. That applies to any product that claims to shift old scale.

## Claims to Walk Away From

Some listings for this category, including some with prices above $100, make claims the independent evidence does not support. Skip any listing that says:

- **"Softens water" or "reduces hardness".** The minerals stay in the water. A seller who says otherwise is counting on you not testing.
- **"Soft, silky water" or "better lather".** Both depend on hardness, which does not change.
- **"Certified to NSF/ANSI 44".** That standard covers ion-exchange softeners. A descaler showing it deserves a hard look at the listing database.
- **"Works on any hardness" or no limit at all.** ScaleBlaster's 19 gpg figure is the kind of number to look for.
- **A 30-day guarantee.** It closes before even a short kettle test can finish.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Anyone who wants soft water.** Spot-free glass, better lather and softer laundry need the hardness removed. If that is the goal, start with [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/) instead.
- **Water harder than about 20 gpg.** ScaleBlaster's own limit is 19 gpg, and halving a heavy scale load still leaves a heavy scale load.
- **Iron or manganese in the water.** No clamp-on device treats them.
- **Homes that can take a tank.** If you can fit a salt-free conditioner, the same independent study measured more than 88 percent scale reduction for crystal-forming media against about 50 percent for electronic devices.
- **Buyers who cannot use the refund.** If the only listing available has a 30-day return policy, or you will not have time to run a test, the main protection this page ranks on is gone.
- **Very soft water.** Below about 3 gpg there is little scale to prevent.

If you still want to try one, the Eddy's year-long window gives you the most room to find out. Whichever you choose, start the kettle test on the day it arrives.
