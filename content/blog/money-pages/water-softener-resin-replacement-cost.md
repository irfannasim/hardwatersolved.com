**Water softener resin replacement cost** is about **$180 to $430 if you do it yourself** and roughly **$480 to $1,130 for a professional rebed**, depending on whether the tank holds 1, 1.5 or 2 cubic feet. The resin itself sells for about $150 to $200 a cubic foot online, and a premium 10% crosslink grade such as ResinTech CG10 was $181.27 for a 1 cubic foot bag when we checked. Labor and the service call are most of a pro's bill. Rebedding makes sense when the control valve is under about 8 years old. Past about 12 years, new resin under an old head is usually a false economy.

Three things stand out from the table.

**The resin is the cheap part of a pro job.** On a 1.5 cubic foot tank, resin is under half of the bill at almost any point in the range. That is the same pattern the site's guide to [water softener resin life and replacement](/blog/water-softener-resin-life-and-replacement/) describes: draining, emptying, cleaning, refilling and recommissioning take hours, and hours are what you pay for.

**DIY saves more than half.** The gap between the two columns is roughly $300 to $700 on every size. That is real money for an afternoon's work, if you can manage the weight.

**Bigger tanks cost more to rebed, but not proportionally.** Going from 1 to 2 cubic feet roughly doubles the resin cost, but the service call stays the same and labor rises only slightly. A 2 cubic foot rebed is still well under the price of a new 64,000-grain system installed.

## How Much Resin Your Tank Holds

Rebed with the volume the tank was designed for. Most tanks carry a size code such as 1054, which means 10 inches across and 54 inches tall. If the label is gone, measure the diameter and the height of the shell.

| Tank size | Resin it normally holds | Common grain rating on the box |
|---|---|---|
| 9 x 48 in (0948) | about 1 cu ft | 32,000 |
| 10 x 54 in (1054) | about 1.5 cu ft | 48,000 |
| 12 x 52 in (1252) | about 2 cu ft | 64,000 |

The box rating assumes about 32,000 grains per cubic foot, which needs a heavy salt dose. At a moderate 6 lb of salt per cubic foot, standard softening resin delivers closer to 20,000 grains per cubic foot. That matters for one decision: if the old unit was running out between regenerations because it was undersized, replacing the resin restores it to being undersized. Resize instead.

**Do not overfill.** Resin needs empty space above it so the bed can lift and loosen during backwash. Filling the tank to the top to "get more capacity" packs the bed, and a packed bed channels and wears unevenly.

## Resin Worth Buying for a Rebed

Both picks are 10% crosslink resin. The extra crosslinking makes the beads tougher against chlorine, which is what slowly degrades softener resin on city water. On unchlorinated well water a standard 8% grade will do, but at these prices the step up is small.

If the Amazon price you see is under $100, it is a short-term sale, not the normal price. [See it on Amazon (B01N7G2UHT)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01N7G2UHT)

CG10 is the safest choice when you want to know exactly what went into the tank. ResinTech publishes the specification, the capacity figure (2.2 meq/mL) and the certifications for it. Buy one bag for a 9 x 48 inch tank. For a 10 x 54 inch tank you need a second half bag, or two bags with the spare kept sealed and damp for a top-up later.

## What a Professional Rebed Quote Should Include

A rebed quote that says "resin replacement, $750" tells you nothing. Ask for these lines:

1. **Resin volume and grade.** Cubic feet and the maker's product name, for example "1.5 cu ft ResinTech CG10". "High capacity resin" is not a grade.
2. **Gravel underbed.** Yes or no. Some tanks use a layer of gravel under the resin, others use a distributor basket alone. Replacing it is cheap.
3. **Distributor tube.** Inspected, or replaced. A cracked distributor is a common reason resin escapes into the plumbing, and a new one is a few dollars while the tank is open.
4. **Tank O-ring and valve check.** A new O-ring at the tank neck, and a note on the valve's condition while it is off the tank.
5. **Labor hours and the service call**, shown separately.
6. **Disposal of the old resin.** Included, or a fee.
7. **Recommissioning.** Backwash, brine refill, a reset of hardness and salt settings, and an outlet hardness test before the installer leaves.
8. **Workmanship guarantee.** How long they stand behind the job if resin appears in the taps or the tank leaks at the neck.

If the quote is for a dealer's proprietary softener, ask whether the resin is a named product you could buy yourself. Some dealers only supply their own media, which takes the DIY column off the table.

## The DIY Job, Honestly Costed

The money saved is in the table. The work is this:

- **Weight.** A 1 cubic foot bag weighs about 55 lb, and a 10 x 54 inch tank full of wet resin and water is far too heavy to tip. You empty it in place with a wet vacuum, or by scooping, or by siphoning.
- **Time.** A half day for a first-timer: bypass and depressurise, unplug, unscrew the valve, empty the tank, rinse, check the distributor, refill, and run through backwash and brine refill.
- **The distributor tube.** It must stay centred and capped while you pour the new resin in through a funnel, or resin goes down the tube and into the valve. A funnel that sits on the tank neck makes this much easier.
- **Disposal.** Old resin is usually rinsed, drained and bagged for household trash where local rules allow. The site's guide to [disposing of old softener resin and components](/blog/disposing-of-old-softener-resin-and-components/) covers the rinse step and the cases, such as radium in well water, where the resin needs a different route.

Stop and get help if the tank threads or neck are cracked, if the valve will not come off without force, or if the old beads are coming out as mush that clogs the vacuum. A damaged tank turns a rebed into a replacement.

## When Rebedding Is a False Economy: The Valve-Age Rule

A rebed restores the bed. It does nothing for the control valve, which is the part with motors, seals, a meter and electronics. That is where the false economy hides.

![Decision chart for water softener resin replacement cost: confirm resin is the fault, fix chlorine or iron upstream, then let control valve age decide between DIY rebed, pro rebed and replacing the whole softener](/diagrams/water-softener-resin-replacement-cost-valve-age-rule.svg "The valve's age, not the resin's, decides whether a rebed is good value")

**The rule:** rebed if the valve is under about 8 years old. Between 8 and 12 years, rebed only if the job costs less than about two thirds of a comparable new unit. Past 12 years, or after any valve repair, replace the softener. Those thresholds follow the crossover in [water softener resin life and replacement](/blog/water-softener-resin-life-and-replacement/).

**A worked example.** A 13-year-old 1.5 cubic foot softener needs new resin. A pro quotes $700 for the rebed. A comparable new 48,000-grain unit with a Fleck 5600SXT valve sells for about $775 on sale at [AFWFilters' own store](https://store.afwfilters.com/water-softeners/48-000-grain/fleck-5600sxt-48-000-grain-water-softener-digital-sxt-metered-whole-house-system-1-5-cubic-foot-48k-max/) ([see it on Amazon, B00OGN3162](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00OGN3162)), and a swap into the existing plumbing adds perhaps $300 to $600 of labor and fittings. Call it $1,075 to $1,375 for a brand-new softener.

| Path | Upfront | If the old valve then fails | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro rebed on the 13-year-old valve | $700 | Valve replacement, roughly $580–$880 installed | **$1,280–$1,580** |
| New softener, swapped into existing plumbing | $1,075–$1,375 | New valve, under warranty | **$1,075–$1,375** |

*Illustrative. The valve figure uses the installed range for a complete metered valve head on the site's repair-cost pages.*

The rebed only wins if the old valve keeps going for years. At 13 years, that is a bet against the odds. At 5 years, with a DIY rebed at $255 to $330, it is the clearly better buy.

The two-thirds test in numbers, on that same tank: two thirds of $1,075 is about $715. A pro rebed at $555 to $930 straddles the line, which is why 8 to 12 years is the "get both quoted" zone. A DIY rebed at $255 to $330 passes easily.

## Costs a Resin Quote Leaves Out

- **Pretreatment.** If chlorine or chloramine broke down the old beads, or iron fouled them, the new bed will fail the same way. A carbon filter ahead of the softener on city water, or iron treatment on a well, belongs in the same budget. Installing new resin on the water that killed the old resin is the most reliable way to pay twice.
- **Shipping on heavy bags.** Compare delivered prices, not list prices.
- **Salt resetting.** After a rebed the capacity is back to new, so the old salt setting may now be wrong. Set it from the resin's capacity at your chosen salt dose, not the box rating.
- **A second visit.** If a pro discovers a cracked distributor or worn valve seals with the tank open, the quote changes on the day. Ask in advance what they charge for those.

The wider budget picture, including what a fresh installation costs when a rebed is not worth it, is in [water softener installation cost](/blog/water-softener-installation-cost/).

## Who Should Not Pay for New Resin at All

- **Anyone who has not confirmed the resin is the problem.** Hard water with no brine draw is an injector, a salt bridge or a setting, not the resin. Test before you buy.
- **Owners of valves over about 12 years old, or with a repair history.** Price a replacement softener first.
- **Owners of undersized units.** New resin restores the original undersized capacity.
- **Homes with untreated chlorine, chloramine or iron.** Fix the water ahead of the softener first, or budget to rebed again in a few years.
- **Owners of dealer systems with proprietary media and a service contract.** Check the contract; the rebed may be covered, or you may be barred from doing it yourself without losing the warranty.

If your valve is young and the tank is sound, buy a 10% crosslink resin from a named maker, put one cubic foot back for every cubic foot that came out, and expect to spend about a third of what a pro would charge.
