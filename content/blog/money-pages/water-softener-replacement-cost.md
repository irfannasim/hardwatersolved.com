**Water softener replacement cost** is about **$910 to $1,570 for a professional like-for-like swap** when the existing loop, drain line and outlet are reused, and about $660 to $915 if you do the swap yourself. That is far below a first installation. If nothing can be reused, the job becomes a full reinstall at roughly $1,310 to $2,970. Sometimes the whole unit does not need replacing at all. A new control valve is about $580 to $880 installed, and a resin rebed $480 to $930. Price only what is actually changing.

The gap between scenarios 3 and 4 is the plumbing. A swap that reuses the loop, drain and outlet avoids roughly $400 to $1,400 of work that a first install pays for. That is why a replacement quote that looks like a first-install quote deserves a question.

Before pricing a whole unit, make sure it is the whole unit that has failed. The site's guide to [signs a water softener needs replacing](/blog/signs-a-water-softener-needs-replacing/) separates the faults a part can fix from the ones that mean the softener is done.

## What a Swap Reuses, and What It Cannot

![Installer kneeling to fit a new electrical outlet in a wall, the line item a full softener reinstall pays for and a like-for-like swap usually reuses](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4981802/pexels-photo-4981802.jpeg)

A like-for-like swap is cheap because most of the first installation is still in the wall. Check each piece before you assume it can stay.

| Part of the old installation | Reusable if… | Cost if it has to be redone |
|---|---|---|
| **Loop** (the pipe tee-ing off the main to the softener) | It is sound, the right size, and outdoor taps still branch off before it | Illustrative $200–$600 |
| **Drain line** | It reaches the new valve, has an air gap at the drain and is not kinked | Illustrative $50–$400 |
| **Electrical outlet** | It is within reach of the new valve's power cord and always on | Illustrative $150–$400 |
| **Brine tank overflow** | The new brine tank's overflow can reach the drain by gravity | Usually just tubing |
| **Bypass valve** | Most new softeners include their own bypass; the old one is replaced | Included, or about $60 |
| **Brine tank and salt** | New systems ship with their own brine tank; leftover salt can be moved across | Included |

*Plumbing ranges from the site's guide to [water softener installation cost](/blog/water-softener-installation-cost/).*

Three things catch people out on a swap:

- **Connection type and size.** The old unit may have a 3/4-inch connection and the new one a 1-inch yoke or bypass. An adapter or a short length of new pipe is a small job, but it should be on the quote.
- **Tank height.** A taller tank may not fit under a low basement beam or beside a water heater.
- **Inlet and outlet sides.** If the new valve's inlet sits on the opposite side, the flex connectors may need to cross. That is fine with enough slack, but it needs checking before the old unit is removed.

## Upgrades Worth Making While the Old Unit Is Out

The cheapest moment to improve a softener is when the old one is already disconnected. These upgrades cost little on top of a swap.

| Upgrade | Extra cost, 2 Oct 2026 | Worth it when… |
|---|---|---|
| Timer valve to a metered valve | Usually $0; most current online units are metered | Always, if the old unit was a timer |
| Standard to 10% crosslink resin | About $10 (AFWFilters, $775 vs $785 on sale) | You are on chlorinated city water |
| 48,000 to 64,000 grain | About $120 (Aquasure Harmony, $630 vs $750) | Hardness or household size means the old unit regenerated very often |
| Standard to fine mesh resin | About $70 (Aquasure Harmony 48k, $630 vs $700) | A well with a little dissolved iron |
| Carbon prefilter | Illustrative $300–$700 plus an hour or two of labor | Chlorine or chloramine shortened the old resin's life |

**Metering is the upgrade that pays.** An old timer softener regenerates on a schedule whether or not you used the water. The EPA notes that softener regeneration can use [25 gallons of water or more a day, up to 10,000 gallons a year](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/cation-exchange-water-softeners), and its guidance points buyers to demand-initiated models that regenerate only when needed. The site's [repair or replace an old water softener](/blog/repair-or-replace-an-old-water-softener/) works through an example where a new metered unit saves about $130 a year even while you pay for it.

**Upgrades that rarely pay on a swap:** oversizing far beyond your daily use, which wastes salt on every regeneration of a big bed, and paying extra for app features if you will never open the app.

## Removal and Disposal of the Old Softener

Removal is a short job and often free. Many installers include haul-away when they fit the replacement; others charge a small fee. Ask for it as its own line, so it does not appear later as a surprise.

If you remove it yourself:

1. Put the old unit in bypass and depressurise it.
2. Unplug it and disconnect the drain, overflow and bypass connections.
3. Drain the mineral tank, then empty the resin. A full tank is far too heavy to carry.
4. Rinse the resin and bag it for household trash, where local rules allow. Resin from water with radium or uranium needs a call to your state first.
5. Take the valve to e-waste, brass fittings to scrap, and the tanks to bulky waste.

## Replacing a Dealer Softener

Culligan, Kinetico, EcoWater, RainSoft, Hague and Rheem dealer systems are not sold on Amazon, and their replacement price is set by the local dealer. Ask the dealer for an itemized quote for a like-for-like replacement, then price the same swap with an online unit and an independent installer using the tables above. Check two things first:

- **Rental or contract terms.** If the old unit is rented or under a service contract, the dealer may own it or need to remove it.
- **Proprietary connections.** Some dealer systems use their own bypass and connectors, so a third-party softener may need new fittings. That is usually a small adapter job, not a new loop.

## Who Should Not Replace Yet

- **Anyone whose fault is a part, not the unit.** A failed motor, seal kit or injector is a repair. Diagnose first.
- **Owners of a softener under about 8 years old with spent resin.** A rebed is the cheaper fix.
- **Homes where the water has changed.** If you now have iron, sulfur or much harder water, price the treatment the new water needs before you buy a like-for-like softener.
- **Homes in areas with salt-discharge restrictions.** Check local rules; a replacement salt-based unit may not be allowed, and a removal rebate may pay more than a new softener saves.

For most owners with a working loop, drain and outlet, the right replacement is a like-for-like metered softener, 10% resin on city water, and a quote that prices only the parts that change.
