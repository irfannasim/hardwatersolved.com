The **best water softener and iron filter combo** depends on which half has to work harder. For dissolved, clear-water iron up to about 5 ppm, a single-tank fine-mesh unit does both jobs: the AFWFilters Iron Pro 2 64k for most families, the Iron Pro 48k for smaller homes and the Iron Pro 80k for big or very hard ones. For rusty water, sulfur or heavier iron, a true two-stage package such as SpringWell's well filter and softener combo puts a separate iron filter ahead of the softener.

Every package has two halves, and both have to suit your water. Below, each one is checked twice: is the iron half right for your iron form, and is the softener half sized for hardness plus the iron it carries? Two of the packages on sale have a half that is smaller than the price suggests.

## The First Half: Is the Iron Half Right for Your Iron?

There are two kinds of combo, and they handle iron in completely different ways.

**One tank (Iron Pro).** Fine-mesh softener resin swaps dissolved ferrous iron for sodium, just as it swaps calcium. AFW says plainly on its [Iron Pro 80k page](https://store.afwfilters.com/water-softeners/80-000-grain/iron-pro-2-5-cubic-foot-80k-on-demand-whole-home-water-softener-with-fine-mesh-resin/) that the system removes "up to 6 ppm of ferrous iron without additional systems required. If ferric iron or higher levels of iron are present a separate iron filter is recommended." It does nothing for sulfur smell, iron bacteria or iron that has already rusted.

**Two stages (SpringWell).** An air injection oxidation filter oxidizes and filters iron, manganese and sulfide first, and a separate softener then removes hardness. SpringWell rates the iron filter for up to 7 ppm of iron, 8 ppm of hydrogen sulfide and 1 ppm of manganese, with a pH range of 6.5 to 10.

So the iron half check comes down to three questions:

| Your water | One tank (Iron Pro) | Two stages (SpringWell) |
|---|---|---|
| Clear when drawn, turns orange in a glass, under 5 ppm | Yes | Yes |
| Orange or cloudy straight from the tap (ferric iron) | No | Yes, within 7 ppm |
| Rotten-egg smell | No | Yes, within 8 ppm |
| Manganese above 1 ppm | Only as extra hardness load | Above its 1 ppm rating |
| Iron bacteria (slime, red gel) | No | No; disinfect first |
| pH below 6.7 | No, per Penn State | Within 6.5 to 10 only |

**If your iron is rusty at the tap or the water smells, the single-tank units are out, whatever their price.** If it is clear, dissolved and under about 5 ppm, the single tank is the far cheaper answer.

## The Second Half: Is the Softener Sized for Hardness Plus Iron?

![Illustrative bar chart of days the softener half of six water softener and iron filter combo packages lasts for a four-person and a six-person well home, with a 3-day minimum line](/diagrams/water-softener-iron-filter-combo-softener-half-days-per-cycle.svg "Iron counts as extra hardness in a single tank, so the same bed runs out sooner")

The two kinds of combo also load their softener differently. A single tank carries the iron as part of its softening job, so you add about 4 gpg for every ppm of iron and manganese. The method, with worked wells, is in [how iron changes water softener sizing](/blog/how-iron-changes-water-softener-sizing/). In a two-stage package the iron is gone before the softener, so the softener sees hardness alone.

The chart uses two example homes at 75 gallons per person per day:

| | Home A: 4 people, 15 gpg, 2 ppm iron + 0.3 ppm manganese | Home B: 6 people, 20 gpg, 3 ppm iron |
|---|---|---|
| Single tank programs | 24.2 gpg, about 7,260 grains a day | 32 gpg, about 14,400 grains a day |
| Two-stage softener sees | 15 gpg, about 4,500 grains a day | 20 gpg, about 9,000 grains a day |

The aim is a bed that lasts at least about three days. Iron that sits on resin for longer than that lowers the next cycle's capacity, so iron softeners are set to regenerate at least every three days anyway. A bed that runs out sooner is regenerating more often than it should and burning salt.

What the chart shows:

- **Home A** is well served by every package except the Iron Pro 32k, at 2.9 days.
- **Home B** pushes every single-tank unit below three days except the 80k, at 3.6 days. A family this size on hard water with iron should buy the 80k or a two-stage package.
- **SpringWell's Core package** lasts 4.7 days in Home A but only 2.3 days in Home B. Its softener half is the smallest 32k unit, which SpringWell sizes for 1 to 3 bathrooms.

SpringWell does not publish resin volume, so the chart uses the industry's usual fills of about 1.0 and 1.5 cubic feet for its 32k and 48k ratings. Treat its bars as estimates.

## Installing a Combo So Both Halves Work

![Stainless steel water pipes fitted with pressure gauges, the readings that show the pressure drop across an iron filter and softener installed in series](https://images.pexels.com/photos/372796/pexels-photo-372796.jpeg)

- **Order:** pressure tank, sediment filter if the well carries sand, iron filter, softener, then the water heater. A single-tank Iron Pro takes the iron filter's and softener's place.
- **No air or chlorine before a single tank.** Anything that oxidizes the iron ahead of an Iron Pro turns it into the ferric iron the resin cannot take.
- **Stagger regeneration.** Set the iron filter and softener at least an hour or two apart, so the pump does not supply both backwashes at once.
- **Program compensated hardness on a single tank,** not the raw hardness, and set the day override to 3 days.
- **Check pressure across both tanks.** Two tanks in series take more pressure than one, so read a gauge before and after if pressure is already marginal.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Wells with iron bacteria.** Slime fouls both resin and filter media. Disinfect first.
- **Iron above about 7 ppm, or manganese above 1 ppm.** Both are beyond what these packages are rated for. Buy a dedicated iron filter rated for your result and a separate softener. Choose the filter by iron level, pH and your pump's backwash flow.
- **Soft water with iron.** If hardness is under about 3 gpg, an iron filter alone is the answer, and the softener half is money wasted.
- **Low-pH water.** Below about 6.5 to 6.7, correct the pH first. Neither kind of combo is designed for acidic water.
- **Owners of a softener with years left in it.** Adding an iron filter ahead of it costs less than replacing both.

The best water softener and iron filter combo is the one where neither half is the weak link. Check the iron half against your iron form and the softener half against your household, and buy the smallest package that passes both.
