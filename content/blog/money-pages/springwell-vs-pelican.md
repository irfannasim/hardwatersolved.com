## Salt-Based: SpringWell SS vs the Pelican Softener

Both are two-tank, metered softeners sold at the 48,000-grain mark. The differences are in what each brand tells you, and what it leaves out.

**The valve.** SpringWell's SS uses an electronic head set up only through SpringWell's phone app. SpringWell does not name the maker. A water treatment dealer's review identifies it as a Chandler Systems Connected Series valve, and SpringWell's bypass is listed as "Bypass Valve CS", so treat that as likely rather than confirmed. Pelican's listing describes a single rotary disc seal and a self-cleaning brine injector, aimed at the two most common service calls: a plugged injector and worn seals. Neither is a Fleck or Clack valve, the two families most local technicians stock. With both brands, repairs run through the brand. How any control valve sequences its cycles is covered in [how a water softener control valve works](/blog/how-a-water-softener-control-valve-works/).

**The resin.** Neither brand publishes resin volume or grade. Several reviews say SpringWell ships 10% crosslink resin, but SpringWell's own page and guide do not. That matters on chlorinated city water, where standard 8% resin breaks down sooner, as set out in [standard vs chlorine-resistant softener resin](/blog/standard-vs-chlorine-resistant-softener-resin/). Ask for it in writing.

**Capacity at a real salt dose.** Neither brand says what salt dose its grain number assumes. A 48,000-grain softener normally holds about 1.5 cubic feet of resin, and both tank sizes fit that. At an efficient 6 lb of salt per cubic foot, that bed delivers about 31,500 grains, not 48,000. The method is in [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/).

| | SpringWell SS4 | Pelican 35921 |
|---|---|---|
| Box rating | 48,000 grains | 48,600 grains |
| Likely resin | About 1.5 cu ft (from tank size) | About 1.5 cu ft (typical for the rating) |
| Usable at 6 lb/cu ft | About 31,500 grains | About 31,500 grains |
| Usable at 15 lb/cu ft | About 46,500 grains | About 46,500 grains |
| Service flow | 13 gpm (pressure drop not stated) | Not stated |
| Mineral tank | 10 x 54 in | Not stated |

At an efficient setting the two are the same softener. The headline 600-grain difference disappears into rounding.

**The setup trap.** SpringWell's installation guide sets the capacity to 48 (48,000) with a 10% reserve and a 10-minute brine refill on the SS4. If that refill delivers an efficient dose, the bed runs out at about 31,500 grains while the valve waits until about 43,200. The household gets hard water for the last days of each cycle. The fix is to lower the capacity setting to match the salt dose, using [how to set softener capacity and reserve](/blog/how-to-set-softener-capacity-and-reserve/). The Pelican listing gives no programming values to check.

## Salt-Free: FutureSoft vs NaturSoft

The salt-free pair is where the brands differed most. Both leave the calcium and magnesium in the water. A hardness test reads the same before and after either tank, as explained in [whether a salt-free system actually softens water](/blog/does-a-salt-free-system-actually-soften-water/).

| | SpringWell FutureSoft | Pelican NaturSoft |
|---|---|---|
| Design | One upflow TAC tank plus sediment pre-filter | Two tanks plus sediment pre-filter |
| Headline claim | "Prevents up to 95% of scale buildup" | "Certified 99.6% effective by DVGW-9191" |
| Evidence published | No protocol, lab or report named | No certificate number given |
| Hardness ceiling | 81 gpg | 75 gpg |
| pH range | 7.0 to 9.5 | Not stated on the listing |
| Iron, manganese, H2S, oil, copper | 0 ppm listed for all five | Iron, manganese, sulfide and tannins removed first |
| Media change | "5+ year lifespan" | First tank about 5 years or 600,000 gallons |
| Pre-filter | Cartridge on its own schedule | Every 6 to 9 months |
| Soak before use | At least 48 hours, then flush both ways | Not stated |

Independent research gives a benchmark. The WateReuse study of salt-free devices, using the German DVGW W 512 heated test, measured more than 88% scale reduction for TAC media. FutureSoft's 95% sits above that, and NaturSoft's 99.6% further still. Both are plausible for the technology. Neither is evidence for the specific product until the brand publishes a report or certificate number you can check.

**The hardness ceilings are maximums, not targets.** Both conditioners work best on moderately hard water and show more spotting as hardness rises. The reasons are in [hardness limits for salt-free conditioners](/blog/hardness-limits-for-salt-free-conditioners/).

**Well water rules both out without pretreatment.** FutureSoft's own installation guide says not to install it on well water without speaking to customer service first. NaturSoft's listings demand iron, manganese, sulfide and tannins be removed before the tanks. On most wells, either one needs a filter in front.

## Guarantees and Warranties Side by Side

![Timeline comparing SpringWell vs Pelican warranties: SpringWell six-month uninstalled return window, lifetime defect cover and 5 or 7 year electronics, NaturSoft 12-year limited warranty](/diagrams/springwell-vs-pelican-return-window-and-warranty-timeline.svg "SpringWell's long cover is for defects; its six-month window is for unopened boxes")

**SpringWell's "6-month money-back guarantee"** is, in its own returns policy, a return window for products "in their original, unused condition (uninstalled and in undamaged packaging)". A 10% restocking fee applies, original shipping is not refunded, and return shipping is the buyer's cost. On an SS4 at about $1,785, a boxed return costs roughly $180 in restocking plus freight both ways.

Installed systems get a separate six-month "Performance Promise": troubleshooting, replacement parts or other remedies. The policy states: "Installed systems are not eligible for return unless determined defective by SpringWell Water." A softener that works but disappoints you is not a refund case.

**SpringWell's lifetime warranty** covers fittings, FutureSoft media, tanks, housings, bypasses, valves and heads against defects "throughout the lifespan of the system". Solid-state electronic heads get a fixed term: 7 years on the warranty page, 5 years on the softener's product-page tab. The warranty belongs to the registered owner at the original address, excludes plumber fees, caps liability at the product's cost, and is void on water outside the system's specification. Keep your water test.

**Pelican's terms** are the gap. NaturSoft's listings state a 12-year limited warranty on tanks, valves, heads, bypass and housings. The salt-based listing gives no warranty text, and with pelicanwater.com offline its returns policy could not be read. Reading a warranty clause by clause is covered in [how to read a water softener warranty](/blog/how-to-read-a-water-softener-warranty/).

## Support After the Sale

For a direct-to-home brand, support is the product as much as the tank is.

- **SpringWell:** phone and app support from the brand. Its SS head is a less common valve, so seals and boards come from SpringWell, not a local supply house. FutureSoft has no valve to fail, but needs a media change at around year five.
- **Pelican:** owners should contact Pentair's residential support, since Pelican was a Pentair brand. Keep proof of purchase and serial numbers. NaturSoft owners should ask a Pentair dealer now whether first-tank media is still supplied.

A softener with a widely stocked valve can be serviced by any water treatment technician. Neither brand's salt-based unit offers that, which is the trade-off of buying a branded head.

## Which to Choose

- **You want soft water, on city water, and value phone support:** the SpringWell SS4, bought direct. Confirm the resin grade and the electronics warranty in writing, and reprogram the capacity to the usable figure.
- **You want soft water at a lower price, or a valve any technician can fix:** one of the Amazon alternatives above. The Fleck-valved system costs less than half the SS4.
- **You want less scale with no salt, on city water within the limits:** FutureSoft is the salt-free unit you can actually order. Budget for a media change around year five.
- **You were set on Pelican:** wait for written confirmation of warranty and media supply from Pentair or a dealer. Until then, buy on the function, salt-based or salt-free, not the brand. The first decision is covered in [salt-based vs salt-free water treatment](/blog/salt-based-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/).

## Who Should Buy Neither

- **Private wells with iron, manganese or sulfur.** Both brands' salt-free units list them as excluded, and SpringWell's warranty calls for pretreatment. Filter first.
- **Anyone who needs third-party proof of performance.** Neither brand had an NSF/ANSI 44 listing or a published scale-test certificate on 2 October 2026.
- **Buyers who want to try a system and return it if they dislike it.** SpringWell's window closes once the unit is installed, and Pelican's terms could not be read.
- **Households on very hard water expecting salt-free results.** Both ceilings are maximums, and spotting from either conditioner rises with hardness. A softener is the better tool.

The verdict on SpringWell vs Pelican: SpringWell is the only live choice of the two, and its SS4 is a sound softener once programmed honestly. Neither brand gives you certification to check. Pelican's NaturSoft had the best-documented warranty of the four, but a warranty is only worth the company standing behind it, and Pelican could not be reached on the day we checked.
