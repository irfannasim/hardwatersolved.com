## Check the Backwash Before the PPM Rating

![Farmhouse standing alone in a field, served by its own private well, where the pump's sustained output decides which air injection iron filter can backwash](https://images.pexels.com/photos/27584808/pexels-photo-27584808.jpeg)

![Bar chart of air injection iron filter minimum backwash flow, 4 to 10 gpm, against example well pump rates of 5 and 8 gpm, with a 2.5 gpm softener for comparison](/diagrams/air-injection-iron-filter-backwash-flow-vs-pump-rate-chart.svg "Bigger tanks and heavier media ask more of the pump than a softener ever does")

An air injection filter cleans itself by running water backwards through the bed, fast enough to lift the media and flush the trapped rust to drain. If the flow is too low, the bed never lifts fully. Iron builds up, channels form, and the filter starts letting iron through within months. That is why the backwash figure is a hard limit, not a guideline.

The numbers in the chart come straight from the sellers:

- **AFWFilters** publishes a [comparison table](https://store.afwfilters.com/air-injection-system-information/) of its four air injection lines: 5 gpm for Silver and Gold, 6.5 gpm for Platinum and 4 gpm for Titanium, all for 1 cubic foot systems. It adds that larger systems need more. Its 1.5 cu ft Gold 15 in a 12-inch tank, for example, needs 6.4 gpm.
- **Oceanic** lists 7 gpm for its 10x54 tank on a Fleck 5600SXT and 10 gpm for its 12x52.
- **For comparison,** a water softener in a 10-inch tank needs about 2.5 gpm on Pentair's spec sheet. An air injection filter of the same diameter needs about twice that.

**Two things stand out.** First, the 10-inch Oceanic needs 2 gpm more than AFW's Silver 10 in the same size tank, so whatever you save on it can cost you the fit. Second, the 12x52's 10 gpm is beyond many domestic pumps. A pump that delivers 8 gpm on a quick test can fall well below that after a few minutes of continuous running.

**How to test your pump.** Fill a 5-gallon bucket from an outside tap with everything else off, time it, and keep repeating for as long as a full backwash runs (the valve manual lists the cycle times). The sustained figure, not the first fill, is the one to compare. [Well pump flow and backwashing](/blog/well-pump-flow-and-backwashing/) walks through both tests and the workarounds if your well falls short.

## Head-Space Air Pocket vs External Venturi

![Cutaway comparing a single-tank air injection iron filter with a head-space air pocket against an older external venturi system with an aeration tank, vent and separate filter tank](/diagrams/air-injection-iron-filter-head-space-vs-venturi-cutaway.svg "One tank and a self-renewing air pocket replaced the vented two-tank setup")

Both designs do the same chemistry. Dissolved iron, manganese and sulfide meet oxygen, turn into particles and are filtered out. They differ in where the air goes in.

**Head-space air pocket (AIO).** The control valve keeps a pocket of compressed air in the top of the tank. Incoming water falls through it, oxidizes, and passes down through the filter media. At each backwash the valve flushes the bed and draws a fresh charge of air back in. Oceanic describes the daily backwash as both cleaning the bed and "adds a fresh air pocket". AFW and Oceanic both describe the single tank as removing the need for a separate tank or pump. Every pick on this page uses this design.

**External venturi.** A venturi fitting on the inlet pipe sucks air into the water whenever it flows. The aerated water needs a tank with a vent to release the excess air, and then a separate filter tank. Oceanic's product pages describe these as the older approach.

What the difference means when you buy:

| | Head-space AIO | External venturi |
|---|---|---|
| Tanks to plumb | One | Two, plus a vent |
| When air is added | At each backwash | Whenever water flows |
| Air in the house lines | Possible if the pocket is overcharged | Common without a working vent |
| Depends on backwash frequency | Yes, the pocket shrinks between backwashes | Less so |
| Space | One tank footprint | Two tanks |

The trade-off for the single tank is the backwash schedule. The air pocket is used up as water passes through it, so these units backwash often: daily for Oceanic, and at least every 3 days for AFW's Silver. A heavy-use household on a 3-day setting can run the pocket down before the next refresh.

## What Air Injection Cannot Do

- **Iron bacteria.** Slime coats the media and valve. Disinfect the well first.
- **Heavy sulfide with no margin.** [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/hydrogen-sulfide-rotten-egg-odor-in-water-wells) notes aeration "is normally not used when hydrogen sulfide concentrations exceed about 2 mg/L" and recommends chlorination for levels above about 6 mg/L. Sellers rate their AIO tanks far higher because the media helps. Those ratings are the best case.
- **A smell that is only in the hot water.** That usually comes from the water heater's anode rod, not the well. It is explained in [why a sulfur smell remains after softening](/blog/sulfur-smell-after-water-softener/).
- **Hardness.** An iron filter does not soften. If the water is hard, the softener goes after it.

## Setting One Up So It Keeps Working

- **Schedule the backwash for the dead of night,** when no one draws water. Any tap running during the cycle steals flow from the bed.
- **Stagger it from a softener.** If both units regenerate at 2 a.m., the pump has to supply both at once. Set them hours apart.
- **Size the drain.** At 7 to 10 gpm, a small drain line or a floor drain that backs up will restrict the backwash.
- **Expect a burst of air at first.** A newly charged air pocket can push air into the house lines. If it persists, the seller can adjust the air draw.
- **Retest after a month.** A lab check of iron and manganese after the filter confirms it is doing what the rating says.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Wells that cannot hold 5 gpm.** Every pick needs at least that.
- **Wells with iron bacteria.** Disinfection comes first, and continuous chlorination may be the long-term answer.
- **Manganese well above iron, on low-pH water.** Manganese needs more oxidizing push than iron. A greensand or chlorine system suits it better. Our separate best iron filters for well water roundup compares those technologies.
- **Hot-water-only sulfur smell.** Replace the anode rod before buying a filter.
- **Low, dissolved iron on hard water.** A fine-mesh softener may handle both on its own.

The best air injection iron filter is the one your pump can backwash, with a rating that covers your test result and some margin. For most wells that is the Silver 10. Move up to the 12x52 only when the iron and sulfide demand it and the pump can feed it.
