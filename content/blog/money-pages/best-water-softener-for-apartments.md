## The Three Renter Blockers, Scored

Most "best softener" lists rank on grain capacity. In an apartment, capacity is rarely the problem. A one- or two-bedroom unit uses little water, and almost any softener has enough capacity for it. ## Check These Lease and HOA Clauses Before You Buy

Read the lease, and in a condo the association's declaration and rules, for these five things before you spend anything:

1. **Alterations or improvements.** Most leases ban changes to plumbing without written consent. A cut-in softener is a plumbing change.
2. **Fixtures and restoration.** Some leases say anything attached becomes the owner's property. Others say you must remove it and repair the damage at move-out. Know which before you install.
3. **Appliances.** Some leases limit tenant-installed appliances, especially ones connected to water. This is where a portable on the washer feed can still need a word with the landlord.
4. **Water damage and insurance.** If a softener leaks into the unit below, the lease and your renter's or condo policy decide who pays. Ask your insurer whether a tenant-installed softener changes anything.
5. **Shared supply lines.** In many buildings, one riser feeds several units. A softener on a shared line softens your neighbours' water and needs the building's approval, not just yours.

## Which Apartment Softener Fits Your Unit

![Decision chart for the best water softener for apartments: no written permission means portable only, no drain means portable or a drain workaround, then space width picks Harmony Lite or EcoPure EP31](/diagrams/apartment-condo-water-softener-renter-blocker-decision.svg "Permission rules out more installs than floor space does")

Size is the last question, not the first. A one-bedroom with two people on 15 gpg water uses about 2,250 grains of hardness a day at 75 gallons per person. Both cabinet picks have far more capacity than that, so set the valve's salt dose low and let it regenerate every week or so. The full method is in [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/).

The drain matters more than people expect. [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/water-softening) puts regeneration water at about 50 gallons a cycle. The drain also has to accept the valve's drain flow; the manual for one compact cabinet we checked, the Whirlpool WHES18, lists 2 gpm. A bathroom sink drain is not a substitute for a standpipe or a floor drain.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Renters who will move within a year** and only want soft water in the shower. A shower filter will not soften, but it is the cheapest way to deal with chlorine. If softening is the point, the portable is the only pick that moves with you.
- **Units on a shared supply line without building approval.** Any cut-in softener here treats water for other people.
- **Buildings that already soften centrally.** Test the tap before buying. Some condo buildings soften the whole supply.
- **Units with very high iron or sulfur smell.** These need treatment upstream that an apartment rarely has room for.

In an apartment, the best softener is the one you are allowed to install and can take with you. Get the written OK first, check the drain second, and measure the space last.
