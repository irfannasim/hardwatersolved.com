**Water softener prices** run from about $500 for a small cabinet to about $1,100 for an 80,000-grain single-tank softener bought online, $1,230 to $1,710 for a twin tank, and $1,785 to $2,320 for a salt-free conditioner. Dealer brands such as Culligan, Kinetico and EcoWater are sold installed, and their reported quotes usually fall between about $1,800 and $6,500, with bundled packages higher. The fairest way to compare those numbers is price per 1,000 grains of capacity you can actually use. On that scale, larger online softeners cost about $20, twin and upflow units about $44 to $46, and a dealer-installed system several times more.

## Prices by Capacity: Bigger Tanks Cost Less per Grain

The grain number on a box is the top of the capacity curve, reached only at a heavy salt dose. At an efficient 6 lb per cubic foot, standard resin delivers about 21,000 grains per cubic foot. That is the figure the site's guide to [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/) uses, and it is the one to price against.

| Box rating | Resin | Usable at 6 lb/cu ft | Typical online price | Price per 1,000 usable grains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32,000 | 1.0 cu ft | about 21,000 | about $760 | about $36 |
| 48,000 | 1.5 cu ft | about 31,500 | about $780 | about $25 |
| 64,000 | 2.0 cu ft | about 42,000 | about $880 | about $21 |
| 80,000 | 2.5 cu ft | about 52,500 | about $1,030 | about $20 |

![Bar chart of water softener prices per 1,000 grains of usable capacity at 6 lb of salt: online single tanks from $36 at 32k to $20 at 80k, twin and upflow about $44 to $46, Culligan installed about $127](/diagrams/water-softener-price-per-1000-grains-usable-capacity.svg "The valve and the brine tank cost the same at every size, so small softeners carry the highest price per grain")

The step from 32,000 to 48,000 grains costs only a little more, yet it adds half as much resin again. The valve, brine tank and bypass cost the same whichever size you buy, so the small unit carries the fixed costs on the least resin. That is the strongest price argument for not buying the smallest size that might just cope.

It is not an argument for buying the biggest. An oversized bed on a household that uses little water sits for days between regenerations, and if the valve is left on a heavy factory salt setting, the salt bill grows with the tank. Size from your daily hardness load, using [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/), then buy the next size up only if the price step is small.

## Water Softener Prices by Type

### Cabinet softeners: about $500 to $750

A cabinet puts the resin tank inside the salt bin. It is the cheapest way into a whole-house softener and the easiest to fit in a closet, but salt storage is small and most use proprietary valves.

**EcoPure EP42, 42,000 grain.** It holds about 200 lb of salt in retail listings, more than most cabinets. EcoPure does not publish its resin volume, so it is left out of the per-grain chart.

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01N5S92DK)

### Single-tank metered softeners: about $760 to $1,050

The standard two-tank design: a resin tank with a metered valve on top and a separate brine tank. These are the price benchmark for everything else, because the Fleck 5600SXT valve is serviced by many sellers.

**AFWFilters Fleck 5600SXT 48,000 grain.** The 1.5 cubic foot high-capacity resin system, the benchmark 48k single tank.

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00OGN3162)

**AFWFilters Fleck 5600SXT 48,000 grain, 10% crosslink resin.** The small upgrade buys resin that tolerates chlorine better, which matters on city water; why is covered in [standard vs chlorine-resistant softener resin](/blog/standard-vs-chlorine-resistant-softener-resin/).

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00YFOTWZG)

### Softener plus iron filter: about $765 to $1,100

Fine mesh resin removes some dissolved iron along with hardness. It costs a few dollars more than standard resin at each size.

**AFWFilters Iron Pro 2, 64,000 grain.** It costs only slightly more than the standard 64k. The seller rates it for ferrous iron up to 6 ppm. Above that, or with oxidized iron, a separate filter is the answer, not a dearer softener; [water softener vs iron filter](/blog/water-softener-vs-iron-filter/) sets out where the line falls.

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004LUJ6L4)

### Upflow premium: about $1,370 to $1,590

Upflow (counter-current) brining gets more capacity from each pound of salt, and premium brands add longer valve warranties and phone support.

**SoftPro Elite 48,000 grain.** Sold in city water and well water versions, it costs well over the AFWFilters 48k with the same 10% resin grade. Upflow regeneration typically recovers 5% to 12% more capacity per pound of salt, which narrows the per-grain gap a little but does not close it. The mechanism is in [upflow vs downflow regeneration](/blog/upflow-vs-downflow-regeneration/).

[Check price on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07KY5SPSJ)

### Twin tank: about $1,230 to $1,710

Two resin tanks alternate, so one is always in service and soft water never runs out during regeneration.

**AFWFilters Fleck 9100SXT twin, 48,000 grain.** It has 1.5 cubic feet per tank, and a twin of this size costs roughly $600 more than one 1.5 cubic foot tank. Priced on the capacity of the tank in service, it comes to about $44 per 1,000 grains. The extra buys continuity and the use of the whole bed, which [single-tank vs twin-tank water softeners](/blog/single-tank-vs-twin-tank-water-softeners/) weighs against the price.

## Online, Big-Box or Dealer: What the Channel Adds

The same capacity costs very different amounts depending on who sells it:

1. **Online, equipment only.** The lowest price. You or a plumber install it, program it and maintain it. Fleck-valve systems can be repaired with parts from many sellers.
2. **Big-box and warehouse packages.** Fixr's [installation cost guide](https://www.fixr.com/costs/water-softener-installation) lists retailer packages from about $1,000 to $2,800 at one big-box chain and $4,000 to $6,000 at a warehouse club. The store arranges an installer; the equipment is usually a retail brand.
3. **Local dealer.** One price for equipment, installation and a service relationship, often with financing on offer. The warranty is longer on paper, but labor after year one is usually the dealer's charge.

![Stack of large cardboard shipping boxes at the entrance of a brick house, how an online water softener and brine tank arrive before a plumber installs them](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6169003/pexels-photo-6169003.jpeg)

Buying online moves two jobs onto you: getting it installed, and getting it programmed to an efficient salt setting. Skip the second and an online softener loses much of its running-cost advantage over a dealer unit.

## The Other Two Halves of the Budget

The purchase price is only one of three numbers. The second is installation. A simple swap into an existing loop is a short job, while a new loop, drain run, outlet and permit can double the labor. Line-by-line figures and why two quotes differ are in [water softener installation cost](/blog/water-softener-installation-cost/).

The third is running cost: salt, regeneration water, electricity, upkeep and an eventual resin change. Over ten years these often add up to more than the softener itself, which is why a slightly dearer, more salt-efficient unit can be the cheaper one to own. The full model is in the [total cost of owning a water softener](/blog/total-cost-of-owning-a-water-softener/).

## How to Check a Price Before You Pay It

1. **Convert the box rating.** Ask for the resin volume in cubic feet, multiply by about 21,000, and divide the price by that figure in thousands. A unit that cannot tell you its resin volume cannot be priced fairly.
2. **Compare like with like.** Equipment-only online prices against dealer installed prices is not a fair test. Add a realistic installation figure to the online price first.
3. **Check what the price leaves out.** Bypass valve, fittings, a sediment prefilter, and on wells an iron filter. Some online prices include a bypass; some list it separately.
4. **Get the dealer price in writing, as a cash price.** A monthly payment is not a price. Ask for the model number, resin volume, salt setting and warranty terms on the same page.
5. **Date the price.** Online softener prices move with sales. A screenshot with the date is the only price you can hold a seller to.

## When the Cheapest Price Is the Wrong Buy

- **City water with chlorine or chloramine.** Pay the $10 to $25 extra for 10% crosslink resin. Standard resin degrades faster, and replacing it costs far more than the upgrade.
- **Well water with iron above the softener's rating, sulfur smell or low pH.** Treat those first. A cheap softener fouled by iron is not cheap.
- **Very small households on moderate water.** A 32k unit costs more per grain, but it is still the right size if a larger bed would sit idle. Price per grain matters most when you need the capacity.
- **Anyone who will not program it.** A dealer unit set up and checked by a technician can cost less to run than an online unit left on its factory salt dose. If you will not change the settings, factor that in before you take the lower price.
- **Homes where softening is not needed at all.** Below about 3 to 7 grains per gallon, many households see little benefit. Test the water before pricing anything.

Prices quoted here move. Use the matrix to set expectations and the per-grain sum to test any quote, then check the live price on the day you buy.
