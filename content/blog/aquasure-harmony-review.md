This **Aquasure Harmony review** comes down to one number: the 48,000-grain model costs about $630 in the box and about $750 to $850 once you have installed it yourself. That price buys a complete two-tank system, with valve, resin tank, resin, brine tank, bypass, drain line and brine line. It does not buy the shutoffs and fittings that connect it, the air gap the drain needs, or the salt to start it. It is a sound budget softener for a homeowner who will install and program it carefully. It is a poor one for anyone who needs a plumber, a certified product or iron removal under warranty.

## What You Still Have to Buy at Install

The manual's own checklist names most of these. The costs are illustrative estimates for a DIY job, not quotes.

| What | Why | Illustrative cost |
|---|---|---|
| Salt, at least two 40 lb bags | The manual asks for 80 lb and 5 gallons of water at start-up | About $20 |
| Shutoff valves, pipe and fittings | To reach the 1-inch connections. Aquasure recommends 1-inch pipe to keep full flow | About $60 to $150 |
| Drain air gap | The manual wants at least 1.5 inches of air gap, never a direct connection to a waste pipe | About $15 to $30 |
| Grounding jumper | Required on copper or galvanized pipe, because the plastic valve breaks electrical continuity | About $15 |
| Pressure-reducing valve | Only if daytime pressure exceeds 80 psi; the valve's limit is 125 psi | $0, or more if needed |
| Carbon prefilter | If chlorine exceeds 2 ppm, or the supply uses chloramine | About $70 extra for Aquasure's bundle with a triple-purpose filter |
| 120 V outlet within 6 ft | The transformer has an 8 ft cord | $0 if one exists |

The carbon line is the one buyers skip. The manual sets a 2 ppm chlorine limit and warns that chlorine and chloramine shorten resin life. Aquasure's own store sells the 48k with a triple-purpose prefilter for about $700, roughly $70 more than the plain unit. On city water that uses chloramine, that is the version to buy.

Two of the most expensive DIY errors happen here: soldering too close to the plastic valve, and connecting the drain without an air gap. The manual asks for at least 6 inches between any solder joint and the valve. Our list of [common water softener installation mistakes](/blog/common-water-softener-installation-mistakes/) covers both, along with the reversed inlet and outlet that catches people on every brand.

## Capacity at Real Salt Settings

Aquasure sells the Harmony by grain rating. The manual programs it from that rating too, through a simple formula: **capacity in grains ÷ hardness in gpg − 75 gallons per person = gallons between regenerations**.

The catch is the capacity figure. The manual's table calls 48,000 grains the "medium salt curve" capacity for the 1.5 cubic foot unit, with an 8-minute brine fill. Industry design tables give standard resin about 21,000 grains per cubic foot at a 6 lb salt dose, 26,000 at 9 lb and 31,000 at a heavy 15 lb dose. Even at that maximum, 1.5 cubic feet delivers about 46,500 grains, not 48,000.

| Model | Resin | Box rating | At 6 lb/cu ft | At 9 lb/cu ft | At 15 lb/cu ft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harmony 32k | 1.0 cu ft | 32,000 | ~21,000 | ~26,000 | ~31,000 |
| Harmony 48k | 1.5 cu ft | 48,000 | ~31,500 | ~39,000 | ~46,500 |
| Harmony 64k | 2.0 cu ft | 64,000 | ~42,000 | ~52,000 | ~62,000 |

*Standard resin in good condition; figures from published per-cubic-foot design guidance, scaled to Aquasure's stated resin volumes. The full table is in our guide to [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/).*

**Worked example.** Four people, 15 gpg water, 48k model:

- From the box rating: 48,000 ÷ 15 − 300 = **2,900 gallons**
- From a realistic 39,000 grains: 39,000 ÷ 15 − 300 = **2,300 gallons**

Program 2,900 and the house runs out of soft water in the last day or two of every cycle. Program 2,300 and it does not.

The manual gives the brine fill in minutes, not pounds, and does not state the refill flow rate, so you cannot read the salt dose off the documents. You can measure it. After a regeneration, check how much the water level in the brine tank rose during the fill. Each gallon of refill water dissolves roughly 3 lb of salt. If your 8-minute fill puts in about 4 gallons, the dose is around 12 lb, or 8 lb per cubic foot, and the realistic capacity sits near the 9 lb column.

## The Aquatrol Valve: What You Give Up

The Harmony's control head is Aquasure's own Aquatrol design, not a Fleck or Clack valve. That has three consequences:

1. **Parts come from Aquasure.** Pistons, seals, boards and injectors for a Fleck or Clack valve are sold by many independent suppliers. Aquatrol parts are not.
2. **Local service is harder.** A plumber or water-treatment dealer who knows Fleck and Clack valves may not know the Aquatrol menus.
3. **The operating envelope is narrower than some.** The manual sets 25 to 125 psi, 32°F to 100°F, and a maximum of 2 ppm chlorine. Hot water must never pass through it.

None of this makes it a bad valve. It makes Aquasure's support line part of the product. Aquasure advertises free US-based technical support for registered products, which is worth using during setup.

## The Warranty: Resolving the 1-, 2- and 5-Year Claims

Three Aquasure sources disagree:

- The **Amazon listing** promises a "5-year extended manufacturer warranty" if you activate the product online within 60 days.
- The **owner's manual** (version 3.4, June 2019) says five years with registration and **two years without**.
- Aquasure's **current [product warranty page](https://aquasureusa.com/pages/product-warranty)** says **one year** without registration, or **five years from purchase** on the valve, electronics and resin if you register within 60 days. The resin tank and brine tank carry a separate ten-year provision.

The current page is the one that governs a purchase today. In practice the rule is simple: **register within 60 days**, and you get five years on the parts that fail.

Read the exclusions as carefully as the term. Labor and freight are not covered. Transfer to a new owner needs Aquasure's written approval and a fee. And **resin and internal valve parts are not covered on systems used to remove iron, manganese, or chlorine above the suggested level**. That clause sits awkwardly beside the fine-mesh models, which Aquasure markets for iron reduction. If you buy a fine-mesh Harmony for iron, assume the resin is not under warranty. Our guide to [reading a water softener warranty](/blog/how-to-read-a-water-softener-warranty/) explains how to check a clause like this before you buy.

## Certification

The Harmony product page makes no NSF or WQA certification claim. NSF's public listings, current as of 30 September 2026, show Aquasure Corporation certified only for reverse-osmosis membrane components under NSF/ANSI 58, with **no Harmony softener under NSF/ANSI 44**, the softener standard. You can [check the NSF listing for Aquasure](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/DWTU/Listings.asp?CompanyName=aquasure) yourself.

That does not mean the softener does not work. It means its capacity and efficiency claims have not been independently verified, which is another reason to program from the realistic capacity rather than the box number. Our guide on [how to verify a softener certification](/blog/how-to-verify-a-softener-certification/) shows what a full listing looks like.

## Sizing: Ignore the Bathroom Counts

Aquasure's own sizing labels are inconsistent. Its store describes the 48k as suiting "4-5 bathrooms, up to 5-6 people". The Amazon listing for the same model says it is "perfectly designed for 3-4 bathrooms". Bathrooms do not use hardness; people and water do.

Size from your hardness and household instead. Multiply people by 75 gallons by your hardness in gpg to get grains per day, then pick the model whose realistic capacity covers about a week. For a family of four on 15 gpg water, that is 4,500 grains a day, which a 48k at a moderate salt dose covers comfortably. The full method is in [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/).

## Which Aquasure Harmony to Buy

- **Most homes on city water:** the [Harmony 48,000](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F175C2R), about $630 at Aquasure's store. Add the triple-purpose prefilter version if your water utility uses chloramine.
- **One or two people, moderate hardness:** the [Harmony 32,000](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F18VG8L), about $540. Its 8 x 44 inch tank also suits tighter spaces.
- **Large households or very hard water:** the [Harmony 64,000](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07F1W1JBD), about $750, with 2 cubic feet of resin.
- **Some iron, on a well:** the [Harmony 48,000 fine mesh with sediment prefilter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0855MSHTH), about $700, but read the iron exclusion above first.

## Who the Aquasure Harmony Is Wrong For

- **Buyers who need it professionally installed.** Once a plumber is involved, the saving over a better-supported valve is small.
- **Anyone who wants certified performance.** There is no NSF/ANSI 44 softener listing.
- **Wells with real iron or manganese.** The warranty excludes the parts that iron damages. Treat iron first, with the approach in our guide to an [iron filter before the softener](/blog/iron-filter-before-water-softener/).
- **Owners who want local, multi-brand parts supply.** The Aquatrol valve is serviced through Aquasure.
- **Homes with chloramine and no budget for carbon.** The resin will not last.

For a confident DIY installer on city water, who registers it on day one and programs it from a realistic capacity, the Aquasure Harmony is a fair deal.
