## Gallons per Regeneration: The Maker's Table vs the Salt You Use

On The Go publishes a clear table for each model. For the 16,000-grain double it lists 3,200 gallons at 5 gpg, 1,066 at 15 gpg and 640 at 25 gpg ([product page](https://www.portablewatersoftener.com/shop/water-softeners/portable-double-standard-water-softener)). Every figure is simply 16,000 divided by the hardness. In other words, the table assumes the bed gives back its full 16,000 grains every cycle.

That is about 30,800 grains per cubic foot of resin, which is the top of the capacity curve and normally needs a heavy salt dose. The regeneration instructions use two boxes of table salt. A standard round box holds 26 oz, so two boxes is about 3.25 lb of salt on 0.52 cubic feet of resin, roughly 6.25 lb per cubic foot. Published design guidance for standard softener resin, summarised in [*Water Conditioning & Purification*](https://wcponline.com/2004/03/14/learn-right-first-time-guidelines-designing-water-softeners/), puts capacity at about 21,000 grains per cubic foot at 6 lb of salt. So two boxes restore roughly 11,000 grains, about two-thirds of the rating.

*Adjusted figures are our illustrative estimates: about 5,500 grains for the 8k (one 26 oz box on 0.25 cu ft) and 11,100 grains for the 16k (two boxes on 0.52 cu ft). Real results vary with resin condition, contact time and how the salt dissolves.*

If you want the maker's numbers, use more salt. Adding a third box to the double raises the dose to about 9.4 lb per cubic foot, which by the same guidance restores roughly 26,000 grains per cubic foot, or about 13,500 grains. The returns shrink with each extra box, which is why the site's guide to [advertised vs usable softener capacity](/blog/advertised-vs-usable-softener-capacity/) treats the box rating as a ceiling, not a target.

## How Often You Will Regenerate on the Road

Daily RV water use varies more than hardness does. We use two illustrative figures: **40 gallons a day** for two people being careful on a full hookup, and **80 gallons a day** for a family of four. Check yours against your fresh-tank gauge or a hose-end meter for a few days.

Two things stand out. First, the 8,000-grain standard turns into an every-few-days chore for a family on hard water. Second, the double covers a typical two-week trip for a couple at 20 gpg on one regeneration. If your route runs through hard-water areas, size for the hardest stop, not the average.

## Getting the Most From a Portable Softener

- **Back-flush after setup.** Run water through to waste for a minute before connecting the RV, so any loose resin fines or salty water do not reach your fresh tank.
- **Regenerate before it runs out, not after.** Keep a note of gallons used. Once hard water reaches the water heater, the scale is already forming.
- **Keep it out of freezing nights.** A frozen tank can crack. Drain it or bring it inside when temperatures drop.
- **Soften only what needs it.** The fresh-water inlet feeds the water heater, shower and taps. There is no need to soften water for rinsing the awning or washing the truck.
- **Store it wet and capped.** The maker includes storage caps. Resin that dries out completely can be damaged.

## Who Should Buy None of These

- **Full-time residents on one site.** At home-sized water use, a portable needs salt every few days. An installed softener with a metered valve costs less to run.
- **Anyone wanting a soft-water exchange service.** Swapping a dealer's tank every month is a different arrangement, covered in [portable exchange tank vs installed softener](/blog/portable-exchange-tank-vs-installed-softener/).
- **Travellers who mostly use soft-water parks.** Below about 3 or 4 gpg there is little to gain.
- **Sites with iron or sulfur smell.** A small portable bed fouls quickly. Filter first, or skip softening at that stop.

Buy on the salt-adjusted gallons, not the box figure. For most RVers that means the 16,000-grain double and a spare box of salt in the bay.
