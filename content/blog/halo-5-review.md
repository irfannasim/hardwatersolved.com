This **HALO 5 review** finds a capable carbon filter attached to a conditioning stage that does not do what most buyers think. The HALO 5 puts four media stages in one tank, mostly carbon, which reduce chlorine, taste and odour. Its fifth stage is a permanent magnetic module, not a softener and not template assisted crystallization (TAC). It removes no hardness. On 1 October 2026 the maker's website was offline and both Amazon listings were unavailable.

So the HALO 5 is now mainly a system you will be quoted by a plumber, not one you can order. What follows covers what each stage does, what it costs to keep running, and how it compares over 10 years with two pairings you can still buy.

## What Is Inside: A Stage-by-Stage HALO 5 Review

All five stages sit in one tank on a backwashing control head. HALO's archived FAQ says its 1-inch units held 1.5 cubic feet of activated carbon and its 1-1/4-inch units held 2 cubic feet.

![HALO 5 review flow diagram of five stages: granular carbon, catalytic carbon, Filter-Ag Plus, garnet and a magnetic module, with hardness unchanged](/diagrams/halo-5-stage-by-stage-media-and-replacement.svg "Four stages filter. The fifth is a magnet, and the water leaves as hard as it came in")

| Stage | Media | What it does | When it is replaced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Granular activated carbon | Reduces chlorine, tastes, odours and some organic chemicals | When chlorine taste or odour returns; HALO put carbon at 400,000 to 800,000 gallons per cubic foot |
| 2 | Catalytic carbon (HALO's "Centaur") | Aimed at chloramine and hydrogen sulfide, which plain carbon handles poorly | With stage 1, at a full re-bed |
| 3 | Filter-Ag Plus | Traps suspended particles; HALO cited about 5 microns | At a full re-bed; backwashing keeps it clean in between |
| 4 | High-density garnet | A heavy support layer that keeps finer media from migrating | Rarely; reused or renewed at a re-bed |
| 5 | HALO ION permanent magnetic module | Magnetic fields intended to stop scale forming; adds or removes nothing | No media to replace |

**Stages 1 and 2 are the real value.** Catalytic carbon matters on city water treated with chloramine, which ordinary carbon removes slowly. HALO said its carbons were certified to NSF/ANSI standards as media, with the Centaur carbon under NSF/ANSI 42. That is a certification of the media as a material. It is not a performance certification of the HALO 5 system.

**Stage 3 is a particle filter, not a disinfection step.** HALO's 2009 product page claimed Filter-Ag Plus removed organisms including E. coli, Giardia and Cryptosporidium. A granular media bed rated around 5 microns is not a disinfection device, and no system certification backed that claim in the sources we found. If your water has a bacteria problem, it needs disinfection, such as UV or chlorination, not a filter medium.

**One oddity worth knowing.** HALO's own 2009 product page also listed an ion-exchange stage regenerated with sodium chloride. The HALO 5 sold on Amazon from 2013 was described as chemical free with no salt. If you are quoted a HALO 5, ask exactly which media are in the tank.

## The Conditioning Stage Is Magnetic, Not TAC

Several distributors describe the HALO 5 as a salt-free conditioner, and buyers often assume that means TAC media, the salt-free technology with the strongest test results. It is not. HALO's own FAQ described the HALO ION module as producing ["multiple reversing polarity magnetic fields"](https://web.archive.org/web/20091205031159/http://www.halowatersystems.com/index.php/categoryblog/88-how-does-the-permanent-magnetic-module-work-to-condition-the-water) that alter calcium and magnesium ions so they "repel one another" instead of forming scale. It "does not add or remove anything from water."

The independent evidence for that class of device is weak. The [WateReuse Research Foundation's study 08-06](https://watereuse.org/watereuse-research/08-06-evaluation-of-alternatives-to-domestic-ion-exchange-water-softeners/) tested softener alternatives under the German DVGW W 512 heated protocol. TAC reduced scale formation by more than 88 percent. Electromagnetic and electrically induced devices reduced it by about 50 percent. Other studies of magnetic devices found no effect at all. The comparison is set out in [water softeners vs electronic and magnetic descalers](/blog/water-softener-vs-electronic-descaler/).

Three practical consequences follow:

1. **A hardness test cannot show it working.** The calcium and magnesium are still in the water. A test strip after the HALO 5 reads the same as before it.
2. **You get none of the soft-water benefits.** Lather, spotting on glass and the feel of the water on skin stay the same, because those depend on the hardness being gone.
3. **There is nothing to maintain, and nothing to verify.** The module has no media to replace, which is convenient, but there is also no home check that tells you whether it is doing anything.

Whether you need scale control or true softening is the first decision, ahead of any brand. [Salt-based vs salt-free water treatment](/blog/salt-based-vs-salt-free-water-treatment/) sets out what each measurably does.

## What Each Stage Costs to Keep Running

The HALO 5's running costs are low, because only the carbon stages wear out and they wear out slowly. HALO's FAQ worked it out using 22,500 gallons per person per year. At the 75 gallons per person per day used across this site, a family of four uses about 109,500 gallons a year.

| Stage | Life for a family of four | Replacement cost basis |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon, stages 1 and 2 (1.5 cu ft in a 1-inch unit) | About 5.5 to 11 years, at 400,000 to 800,000 gallons per cubic foot | Catalytic carbon lists at about $283 per cubic foot and $163 per half cubic foot at AFW's store, so about $446 for 1.5 cubic feet of media alone |
| Filter-Ag Plus and garnet | Usually renewed at the same re-bed | Small extra media cost plus labour |
| Magnetic module | No consumable | $0 |
| Backwash water | Every backwash sends water to drain | Not published for the HALO 5 |

HALO's archived FAQ answer on media life is the source for the carbon figures. It also advised testing the water every year, or simply waiting until chlorine taste or odour returns. With HALO's own replacement media no longer sold, a re-bed means generic carbon of equivalent grade, installed by a dealer or a confident DIYer.

The same pattern of "maintenance-free" claims hiding a real replacement schedule applies to most salt-free systems. The general schedule, and how to tell when media is spent, is in [salt-free water conditioner maintenance](/blog/salt-free-water-conditioner-maintenance/).

The pattern is clear. At the low end of its price range a HALO 5 is cheap to own, but it buys filtration plus the weakest kind of scale control. At the high end it costs more over 10 years than a softener and carbon pair that removes the hardness completely. The salt-free bundle sits between them, with scale-control media that is at least a different technology from a magnet.

Installation is excluded from every column. A HALO 5 is one tank to plumb in, and each pairing is two or three. Expect the pairings to cost a little more to install.

## The Water the HALO 5 Is Not Built For

- **Hard water where you want soft-water results.** Spotting, soap scum, lather and dry skin are hardness problems, and the HALO 5 leaves the hardness in.
- **Well water with bacteria.** It is not a disinfection system, whatever older marketing said about Filter-Ag Plus.
- **Iron and manganese.** Catalytic carbon can handle a little, but iron above trace levels calls for dedicated iron treatment ahead of anything else.
- **Water heaters and tankless units with hardness terms in their warranty.** Some makers set a hardness limit. Check yours before relying on a device that leaves the hardness in.
- **Anyone who wants to verify results.** With no hardness change to measure, you are relying on the claim.

## Warranty: What the Paper Said, and Who Stands Behind It Now

HALO's archived [limited lifetime warranty](https://web.archive.org/web/20091205175415/http://www.halowatersystems.com/index.php/warranty) promised one replacement mineral tank for a manufacturing defect, valve parts within five years of manufacture, and components available for the life of the system. It excluded all water-conditioning media, because media life "is highly reliant on influent water conditions". Installer guides published since then describe the warranty variously as 5 years or 10 years.

The terms matter less than the question of who would honor them. With the manufacturer's site gone, assume the effective warranty is whatever the installer personally commits to in writing.

## Who Should Not Buy a HALO 5

- **Anyone who wants soft water.** Buy an ion-exchange softener and add carbon if you also want chlorine removed.
- **Buyers who need a supported product for 10 years.** The maker's site is offline and the Amazon listings are dead.
- **Households relying on it for safety.** It is not a disinfection system and carries no system-level health certification.
- **Anyone comparing it with TAC salt-free media.** The HALO 5's conditioning stage is magnetic, and magnetic devices test well below TAC.

## The Verdict

The bottom line of this HALO 5 review: its carbon stages are a sound whole-house filter for chlorine, chloramine, taste and odour, and its running costs are low. Its conditioning stage is a permanent magnet that removes no hardness, backed by the weakest evidence in salt-free treatment. Add a vanished manufacturer and no Amazon availability, and it is hard to recommend over two tanks you can buy today. If you want soft water, a salt softener and carbon filter pair costs about the same over 10 years and actually removes the hardness. If you only want filtration and some scale control without salt, a salt-free bundle with a named media and a carbon stage is the more supportable choice.
