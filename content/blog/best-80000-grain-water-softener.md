## Who an 80k Single Fits

Daily hardness load is people × 75 gallons × gpg. Add about 4 gpg per 1 ppm of dissolved iron, as set out in [how iron changes softener sizing](/blog/how-iron-changes-water-softener-sizing/). A single 80k delivering 50,000 grains, with one day held back as reserve, regenerates every 3 to 8 days when the load is between about 5,600 and 12,500 grains a day.

## The Backwash Your Well Must Deliver

Backwash flow rises with the area of the resin bed, not the grain rating. The [WC&P softener design guide](https://wcponline.com/2004/03/14/learn-right-first-time-guidelines-designing-water-softeners/) asks for enough flow to expand the bed by at least 50 percent, at about 4.2 gpm per square foot in 50°F water rising to 5.5 in 68°F water. A 13-inch bed has about 0.92 square feet, so it needs roughly 3.9 to 5.1 gpm. AFW's published 4.6 gpm sits in the middle of that range. A 10-inch bed needs about 2.3 to 3.0 gpm, and AFW publishes 2.7 for its twin.

On city water none of this matters. On a well, the flow has to be held for the whole backwash and rinse, not just a minute. Penn State Extension's fact sheet on [low-yielding wells](https://extension.psu.edu/using-low-yielding-wells) explains why a quick bucket test flatters a weak well: the first few minutes come from water stored in the casing, and the well's own yield may be far lower. The two-test method, a pump-rate bucket test and then a sustained run at the backwash rate, is set out in [well pump flow and backwashing](/blog/well-pump-flow-and-backwashing/).

If the sustained test fails at 4.6 gpm but holds at 2.7, the twin is the answer. If it fails at 2.7 too, no backwashing softener of this size suits the well as it stands. The options are covered in [sizing a softener for a low-yield well](/blog/sizing-a-softener-for-a-low-yield-well/).

## Single or Twin: The Rule

![Decision chart for choosing between a single 80,000 grain water softener and a twin tank based on well backwash flow, daily hardness load and tolerance for hard water during regeneration](/diagrams/80000-grain-water-softener-single-or-twin-tank-rule.svg "Three checks; if all pass, the single 80k wins on price and flow")

Choose a **single 80k** when all three of these hold:

1. **The well can sustain about 4.6 gpm** for 20 minutes or more, or you are on city water.
2. **Your daily load is between about 5,600 and 12,500 grains**, iron-adjusted.
3. **Two hours of untreated water overnight is acceptable**, which it is for most households that are asleep at 2 am.

Choose a **twin** if any one of them fails. Choose nothing at this size if your load is under about 5,600 grains a day. A 64k, or the size worked out in [how to size a water softener](/blog/how-to-size-a-water-softener/), will serve you better.

## Who Should Skip an 80k Altogether

- **Households under about 5,600 grains a day.** An 80k would sit more than 8 days between regenerations, wasting salt held in reserve and leaving the resin idle.
- **Wells that fail a sustained 2.7 gpm test.** No backwashing softener of this capacity suits them without storage or a smaller design.
- **Water with visible rust, iron bacteria or iron above about 6 ppm.** Put an iron filter first, and remember it adds its own backwash demand.
