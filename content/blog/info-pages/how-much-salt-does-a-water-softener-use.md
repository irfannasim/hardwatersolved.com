**How much salt does a water softener use?** Most homes use between about 10 and 80 pounds a month, which is anywhere from a quarter of a bag to two 40 lb bags. A family of four on moderately hard water, with the softener at an efficient setting, typically lands near 30 to 35 lb a month. Your own figure depends on three numbers: water use, hardness and salt dose.

Work those out and you get more than a shopping estimate. Compared against what you actually pour in, the expected figure is one of the quickest ways to spot a softener that is mis-set or failing.

## The Three Numbers That Set Salt Use

A softener uses salt to put back the capacity that hardness used up. So salt use follows the hardness removed:

> **Pounds of salt a month = gallons a day × hardness (gpg) × 30 ÷ grains per pound of salt**

1. **Gallons a day through the softener.** The EPA's [WaterSense figures on household water use](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/how-we-use-water) put the average family at more than 300 gallons a day at home, with about 70 percent of it used indoors. For softener arithmetic, **70 gallons per person per day** is a sound starting point if outside taps bypass the softener, as they usually should. Better still, read your water bill or the softener's meter.
2. **Hardness in grains per gallon**, from a recent test. If your report gives mg/L or ppm, divide by 17.1. Add any iron allowance your manual asks for.
3. **Grains per pound of salt at your salt dose.** This is the one most estimates skip, and it swings the answer by nearly half.

That third number comes from the manufacturer's capacity table. The [Hellenbrand ProMate 6.0 manual](https://www.hellenbrand.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/Manual-Owners-PM6-800198.pdf) prints capacity at four doses, and dividing capacity by salt gives:

| Salt dose (per cu ft of resin) | Grains removed per lb of salt | Salt use relative to the 6 lb setting |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3 lb (efficient) | 4,228 | 0.90× |
| 6 lb (low, factory setting) | 3,822 | 1.00× |
| 10 lb (medium) | 2,806 | 1.36× |
| 15 lb (high, the "grain rating") | 2,154 | 1.77× |

The same house on the same water uses **77 percent more salt** at the high setting than at the low one. Why the box rating sits at the least efficient dose is covered in [how to set the salt dose on a water softener](/blog/how-to-set-the-salt-dose-on-a-water-softener/).

## How Much Salt Does a Water Softener Use? The Calculator

Worked at 70 gallons per person per day and the 6 lb factory dose (3,822 grains per pound):

**Pounds of salt a month**

| People | 5 gpg | 10 gpg | 15 gpg | 20 gpg | 25 gpg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 11 | 14 |
| 2 | 5 | 11 | 16 | 22 | 27 |
| 3 | 8 | 16 | 25 | 33 | 41 |
| 4 | 11 | 22 | 33 | 44 | 55 |
| 5 | 14 | 27 | 41 | 55 | 69 |
| 6 | 16 | 33 | 49 | 66 | 82 |

**Adjust for your valve:**

- **Medium dose (10 lb per cu ft):** multiply by 1.36.
- **High dose (15 lb per cu ft):** multiply by 1.77.
- **Timer valve:** the table does not apply. A timer uses its dose on every scheduled regeneration, so monthly salt = regenerations a month × pounds per regeneration.
- **Potassium chloride:** add 12 to 20 percent (see the FAQ below).

**Treat the result as a floor, not a target.** A metered valve regenerates with some capacity still unused, the reserve it holds back so you never run hard. Worked through Hellenbrand's table for a 1 cubic foot unit holding back one day's water, real use came out about **11 percent above** the simple estimate. Manufacturers also note that efficiency in a home is typically lower than the laboratory figure.

**A check against the manufacturer's rule of thumb.** Hellenbrand's manual says a family of four uses about 60 lb a month. The calculator gives 33 lb at 15 gpg on the factory dose, and 58 lb at the high dose. The manual's figure is what the high setting uses.

### A second route: from the valve itself

If your valve shows a regeneration count or history, you can skip the household arithmetic:

> **Salt a month = regenerations a month × salt dose per regeneration**

A 1 cubic foot bed set to 6 lb that regenerates five times a month should use about 30 lb. This route is useful because it checks the brine side directly. If you are pouring in far more than regenerations × dose, the valve is making more brine than it was told to.

## Turning Your Figure Into a Refill Schedule

| Expected use a month | A year | 40 lb bags a year | Typical routine |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 lb | 120 lb | 3 | Check monthly, add a bag every 3 to 4 months |
| 20 lb | 240 lb | 6 | A bag about every 2 months |
| 30 lb | 360 lb | 9 | A bag about every 6 weeks |
| 40 lb | 480 lb | 12 | A bag a month |
| 60 lb | 720 lb | 18 | A bag every 3 weeks, check every 2 |
| 80 lb | 960 lb | 24 | A bag every 2 weeks, or two at a time |

Manufacturers phrase the fill level differently. [Morton's potassium chloride directions](https://www.mortonsalt.com/home-product/morton-potassium-chloride-pellets/), for example, say keep the tank at least half full. Hellenbrand says fill to about three-quarters with a minimum of 12 inches of salt. Neither means "fill to the rim." A tank kept brim-full makes bridging more likely and hides changes in how fast the salt is going down. The working band and how to read it are in [how much salt should be in a brine tank](/blog/how-much-salt-in-a-brine-tank/).

**Log it.** Write the date and number of bags on a card taped to the lid. After three or four refills you have your household's real monthly figure, and that is the number to compare against.

To put a price on the annual figure, see [how to estimate annual water softener salt cost](/blog/annual-water-softener-salt-cost/).

## Three Homes, Worked Through

The calculator is simple. Real valves add a few wrinkles, and these three examples show the ones that matter most. All are calculated from Hellenbrand's capacity table, not measured.

**1. One person on city water at 8 gpg, 1 cubic foot softener at the 6 lb setting.**
The arithmetic says 1 × 70 × 8 × 30 ÷ 3,822 = **about 4.4 lb a month**. The softener, though, can soften 22,930 ÷ 8 = 2,866 gallons between regenerations, which is about 41 days of use. Most valves will not wait that long. A day override of 14 days forces a regeneration every two weeks, about 2.1 a month at 6 lb each, so **about 13 lb a month**. For small households, the override setting, not the water, sets salt use. Lowering the dose, or accepting the override, are both reasonable. Just expect the higher figure.

**2. Four people on well water at 18 gpg, 1.5 cubic foot softener at 9 lb per regeneration (6 lb per cubic foot).**
Hardness removed is 4 × 70 × 18 = 5,040 grains a day, so the calculator gives **about 40 lb a month**. The bed holds 34,400 grains, a little under seven days of use. Holding back a day's reserve, it regenerates about every six days: 5 regenerations × 9 lb = **about 45 lb a month**, 14 percent over the simple figure. That is well inside the normal band.

**3. Two people at 12 gpg with a timer valve set to every fourth night at 10 lb.**
The calculator says **about 13 lb a month** is all the hardness needs. The timer does not know that. It regenerates 7.5 times a month at 10 lb: **75 lb a month**, almost six times the expected figure. Nothing is broken. The valve is doing what it was told. This household would save more than 700 lb of salt a year by recalculating the interval, or by moving to a metered control. How much more an efficiency-rated unit saves on top of that is smaller than it sounds: [the Morton review puts it at about 100 lb a year against a well-set metered softener](/blog/morton-water-softener-review/).

## Using the Expected Figure as a Fault Detector

Divide your logged monthly use by the expected figure. The ratio tells you where to look.

![Scale showing how much salt a water softener uses compared with the expected figure, with bands for too little, normal, check settings and fault](/diagrams/water-softener-salt-use-actual-vs-expected-fault-bands.svg "The ratio of actual to expected salt tells you where to look first")

**Under about 0.6 times expected.** First, test the softened water.

- **Water hard:** the salt is not getting used. A salt bridge, mushy salt blocking the brine well, a blocked injector or brine line, or a valve left in bypass. Start with [why a water softener is not using salt](/blog/water-softener-not-using-salt/).
- **Water soft:** the estimate was high. You use less water than 70 gallons per person, someone is away, or more taps bypass the softener than you thought. Nothing is wrong.

**About 0.6 to 1.5 times expected: normal.** Reserve losses, iron, laundry weeks and guests all live in this band.

**About 1.5 to 2 times expected: check the settings.** Hardness entered higher than the test result, a dose set to maximum, a day override forcing regeneration every few days, or simply more people at home than there were.

**Over about 2 times expected: something is wrong.** The usual causes, roughly in order of how often they turn up:

1. **Hardness entered in ppm on a valve that expects gpg.** It tells the softener the water is about 17 times harder than it is.
2. **A brine tank that keeps filling,** from a sticking brine valve or a failed float. Look for water high in the salt or trickling from the overflow.
3. **A leak the meter counts,** such as a running toilet. With every tap off, the softener's flow indicator should be still.
4. **A timer valve** regenerating on a schedule set for a busier house than yours.

These band edges are working rules of thumb, not manufacturer limits. The full diagnosis for the top band is in [why a water softener uses too much salt](/blog/water-softener-using-too-much-salt/).

## What Changes Salt Use Over Time

- **Seasons.** Summer showers, laundry and guests raise use. If irrigation or a pool top-up runs through the softener, summer use can double.
- **Hardness drift.** Well water hardness can move with the seasons and over years. Re-test annually and update the setting.
- **Iron.** Dissolved iron uses resin capacity just as hardness does. If iron appears or rises, salt use goes up with it once the setting is corrected.
- **Resin aging.** Older resin holds less capacity, so a metered unit regenerates more often. Slowly rising salt use over several years, with nothing else changed, is one sign.
- **Settings reset.** A power cut or a service visit can put a valve back to factory settings. A sudden change in salt use deserves a look at the screen.

## What Salt Use Cannot Tell You

- **Whether the water is soft.** A softener can use salt normally and still leak hardness through a fouled bed or a bad valve seal. Only a hardness test on softened water answers that.
- **Anything within a few weeks.** One or two refills are too few. Brine tanks have no gauge, and the level depends on how the salt has settled. Judge on a month or more.
- **Anything about sodium in your drinking water.** That depends on how much hardness is removed, not on how much salt you buy. A leaner salt setting does not reduce it.
- **Anything about a salt-free conditioner.** Those use no salt, and they do not remove hardness either.
